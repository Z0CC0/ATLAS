internal knowledge

Before choosing an architecture, find out what your team must be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today. That question is not technical, and it decides the rest.

**Map your monolith first:**

Which parts actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

**How you migrate:**

Avoid a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Use progressive strangling instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until the new one is verified.

**Target architecture:**

Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it.

With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

A modular monolith is the option nobody mentions. It has sharp internal boundaries and one release. You can still extract a module the day you genuinely need to.
