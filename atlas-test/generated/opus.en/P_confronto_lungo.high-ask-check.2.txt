internal knowledge

Use local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere. That way the application never waits on a log write.

The rule that always holds: writing the log must stay out of the request's critical path.

**Files:** no cost, no dependency. Searching means grep, and across several machines it doesn't exist, so you have to connect to each one. You have to configure rotation by hand, or the disk fills.

**Managed service:** instant search, alerts and automatic retention. You pay by volume, though, and at a thousand requests a minute that volume grows fast. The logs also leave your own infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they're sent.

**Database:** searching is familiar and convenient. Writing logs into the application's own database puts two very different workloads in competition. Under load, the logs fill caches and take connections that real requests needed.
