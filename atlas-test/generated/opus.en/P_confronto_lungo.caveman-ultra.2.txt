Use local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere. At 1,000 requests/minute, the application then never waits on a log write.

One rule holds whatever you pick: writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.

**Files alone**
- Zero cost, no dependency.
- Searching means grep.
- You have to configure rotation by hand, or the disk fills.
- You can't search across several machines. You have to connect to each one.

**Managed service**
- Search is instant, and you get alerts and automatic retention.
- You pay by volume. At 1,000 requests/minute, volume grows fast.
- The logs leave your own infrastructure. Strip personal data before they go.

**Database**
- Searching is familiar and convenient.
- Writing logs into the application's own database puts two very different workloads in competition.
- Under load, the logs fill caches and connections that the real requests needed.

**What to do:** have the application write to local files with rotation configured. A collector reads those files and ships them to wherever you search them. If you send them to a managed service, strip personal data in the collector and keep an eye on volume cost. Don't send them to the application's database.
