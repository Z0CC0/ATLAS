internal knowledge

**Start with the non-technical question: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today?** Architecture follows from that answer.

Next, find which parts of the monolith actually change. Splitting out stable parts gains nothing.

Then map transaction boundaries. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Don't rewrite everything in parallel with the old system. That almost always fails: the old system keeps changing, and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until each new piece is verified.

Count your developers before choosing microservices. Each service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person looking after it. Below five developers, that operational cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

**Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions.** It gives you sharp internal boundaries and a single release. You can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
