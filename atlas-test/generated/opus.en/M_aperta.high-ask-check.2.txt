**First question isn't technical:** what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Architecture follows from that answer.

Then map monolith:

Which parts actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Where transaction boundaries sit. Transaction spanning two services becomes distributed problem.

Don't rewrite in parallel. Full rewrite beside old system almost always fails: old one keeps changing, copy never catches up.

Strangle progressively instead: router in front, move one piece at a time, old system stays authoritative until new piece verified.

Weigh microservices' operating cost: every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person looking after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds benefit.

**Consider modular monolith**, option nobody mentions: sharp internal boundaries, one release, and you can extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
