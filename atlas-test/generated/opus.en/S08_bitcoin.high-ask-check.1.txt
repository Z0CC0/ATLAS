internal knowledge

Bitcoin grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts, b-money and bit gold, did not solve how to order transactions.

In 2008 a paper appeared, "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System", signed Satoshi Nakamoto. The name is a pseudonym, never identified.

It tackled double spending without a central body keeping the ledger.

Its answer was a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting the past is expensive, because the work would have to be redone for every later block.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It carries the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout, which works as both a timestamp and a statement of intent.

The block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. Total issuance tends to 21 million and goes no further.

The first documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011. Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
