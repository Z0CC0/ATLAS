internal knowledge

Eventual consistency means that after a write, replicas don't agree right away. If writes stop, they converge within a finite time.

Why you should care: it affects anyone who reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it. Within one region, replicas usually converge in under a second. Across regions it takes seconds.

The usual fix is to read from the primary replica, but only for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

The trade is against availability. A system that demands strong consistency has to refuse requests when its replicas can't talk to each other. So eventual consistency isn't a defect to eliminate. It's a choice, and most systems that scale make it on purpose.

Where it's unacceptable, the cases are few and easy to recognise: account balances, seat availability, and uniqueness of an identifier.
