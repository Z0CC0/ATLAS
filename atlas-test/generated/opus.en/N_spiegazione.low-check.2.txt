internal knowledge

Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree right away, but they converge within finite time once writes stop.

**Why you should care:** it hits whoever reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it. That looks like a bug to them, even though system works as designed.

Why systems accept it: the trade is against availability. A system demanding strong consistency has to refuse requests whenever replicas can't talk to each other. Most systems that scale choose eventual consistency deliberately. So treat it as a choice, not a defect to eliminate.

How long the gap lasts: across replicas in the same region, usually under a second. Across regions it rises to seconds.

Common fix for the comment problem: send reads to the primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone. The writer sees their own change right away, and everyone else keeps reading from replicas.

Where you can't accept it: the cases are few and easy to spot. Account balances, seat availability, and uniqueness of an identifier. For these, use strong consistency and accept the cost to availability.
