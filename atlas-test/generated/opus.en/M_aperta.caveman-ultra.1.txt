Decide by one question first, and it is not technical. What must team be able to do one year from now that it cannot do today? Pick architecture that answers that.

**1. Find which parts change.**
Map which parts of monolith change often. Stable parts gain nothing from split. Leave them alone.

**2. Find transaction boundaries.**
Find where transactions start and end. Transaction spanning two services becomes distributed problem. Draw service lines so no transaction crosses them.

**3. Do not rewrite everything in parallel.**
Full rewrite beside old system almost always fails. Old system keeps changing, copy never catches up.

Use progressive strangling instead:
1. Put router in front of monolith.
2. Move one piece at a time.
3. Keep old system authoritative until new piece is verified.

**4. Count operational cost.**
Microservices cost more to run. Each service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and one person who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds benefit.

**5. Consider modular monolith.**
Option nobody mentions. Sharp internal boundaries, one release. You can extract one module the day it is truly needed. Good fit for small team, or when you are unsure which parts will change.
