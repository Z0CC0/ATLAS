## Where it came from

Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene. People there had already tried to build digital money, with designs like b-money and bit gold. Those attempts never solved one core problem: **ordering**, meaning how everyone agrees which transaction came first.

## The paper (2008)

In 2008 a paper appeared titled **"Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System."** It was signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym whose owner has never been identified.

The paper took on **double spending**: how to stop someone spending the same digital coin twice when no central body keeps the ledger.

## The fix: a chain of blocks plus proof of work

- Transactions go into **blocks**, and the blocks are linked in a **chain**.
- The order of the chain is set by **proof of work**, a computational effort each block needs.
- Changing an old block means redoing the proof of work for that block **and for every block after it**. That makes rewriting the past expensive, so the ordering holds.

This is the piece that b-money and bit gold were missing.

## The first block (3 January 2009)

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That headline does two jobs:

1. **Timestamp:** it proves the block could not have been made before that day.
2. **Statement of intent:** it points at the kind of centralised financial system Bitcoin was meant to be an alternative to.

## How new bitcoin are issued

- Each block starts out paying a **reward of 50 bitcoin**.
- The reward **halves every 210,000 blocks**, which is about every four years.
- Because of the halvings, the total supply approaches **21 million** and never goes past it.

## First real use (2010)

The first documented commercial transaction happened in 2010: **two pizzas bought for 10,000 bitcoin**.

## Satoshi steps away (2011)

In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part publicly. The bitcoin tied to Satoshi have **never been moved**.

**In short:** earlier cypherpunk designs couldn't agree on transaction order. Satoshi's 2008 paper solved that with proof of work on a chain of blocks. The network started in January 2009 with a capped supply, saw its first real purchase in 2010, and was running without its creator by 2011.
