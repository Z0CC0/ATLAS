## The short version
In 2008 someone calling themselves Satoshi Nakamoto published a design for digital money that needs no bank to keep the books. In January 2009 the network went live. Satoshi left in 2011, and Bitcoin has kept running without them.

## Where the idea came from
Bitcoin grew out of the cypherpunk scene, a community working on cryptography as a tool for personal freedom. People there had tried to build digital cash before, with designs such as **b-money** and **bit gold**. None of them solved one key problem: **ordering**, meaning how everyone agrees on which transaction happened first.

## The 2008 paper
In 2008 a paper appeared titled **"Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System."** It was signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym that has never been linked to a real person.

The paper took on **double spending**. Digital money is just data, so what stops someone from spending the same coin twice? Normally a central body keeps the ledger and checks this. Satoshi wanted to remove that central body.

## How it solves the problem
- Transactions are grouped into **blocks**, and the blocks are linked into a **chain**.
- The chain is put in order by **proof of work**, a costly computation that has to be done for each block.
- Rewriting the past is expensive. If you change an old block, you have to redo the proof of work for that block and **for every block after it**.

So the order of transactions becomes something nobody can cheaply fake. That is the ordering problem the earlier attempts never solved.

## The first block (3 January 2009)
The first block was created on **3 January 2009**. It contains the headline of a *Times* article about a **bank bailout**. That headline does two jobs:
1. **Timestamp:** it proves the block could not have been made before that date.
2. **Statement of intent:** it points to the kind of financial system Bitcoin was meant to be an alternative to.

## How new bitcoin are created
- Whoever produces a block earns a reward. The reward started at **50 bitcoin**.
- It **halves every 210,000 blocks**, which is roughly every **four years**.
- Because of the halvings, the total supply gets closer and closer to **21 million** but never goes past it.

## First real use (2010)
The first documented commercial transaction took place in **2010**: **two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin**.

## Satoshi disappears (2011)
In **2011** Satoshi stopped taking part publicly. The bitcoin linked to Satoshi have **never been moved**. The creator is gone, and the system they designed keeps working without them, which is what it was built to do.
