internal knowledge

The question that decides it isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Architecture follows from that answer.

Before drawing any lines, map two things in the monolith.

First, which parts actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split out.

Second, where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction that spans two services becomes a distributed problem.

On how to migrate, don't run a full rewrite in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up. Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until each new piece is verified.

On the target shape, microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

The option nobody mentions is a **modular monolith**. You get sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can pull a module out as its own service the day you genuinely need to.
