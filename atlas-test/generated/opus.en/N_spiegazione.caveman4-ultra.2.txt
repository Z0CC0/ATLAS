**Eventual consistency:** after a write, the replicas don't agree right away. If writes stop, they all converge within a finite time.

**Why it exists:** it's a trade against availability. If a system demands strong consistency, it has to refuse requests whenever the replicas can't talk to each other. Eventual consistency keeps answering instead.

**How long it takes:** usually under a second between replicas in the same region. Between regions it rises to seconds.

**Where it hurts you:** reading right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads the page, and the comment isn't there.

**Common fix:** send reads to the primary replica only for the session that just wrote. Everyone else keeps reading from replicas.

**Why you should care:** it isn't a defect to eliminate. It's a choice, and most systems that scale make it on purpose. Your job is to spot the few places where it's unacceptable. They're easy to recognise:
- account balances
- seat availability
- uniqueness of an identifier

Use strong consistency there and accept eventual consistency everywhere else.
