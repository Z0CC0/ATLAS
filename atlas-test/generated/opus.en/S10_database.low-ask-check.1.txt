internal knowledge

Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when one specific workload needs it and you have numbers to show it.

**Why Postgres:** it handles all four workloads acceptably, though none outstandingly. It gives real transactions, and on orders those are not negotiable. That alone decides where authoritative data lives.

Its limits tell you when to look again. Built-in text search works well enough below a million documents and weak above. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

**Alternatives:**

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. Nothing gained over Postgres.

MongoDB: catalogue gets easier with variable schemas. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, so poor fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search, with relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. Not an authoritative store: it has to be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

If you grow past Postgres's limits, the usual setup is Postgres as authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. Real cost of specialised systems isn't licence but time of people keeping them up. Add them one at a time, only when numbers demand it.
