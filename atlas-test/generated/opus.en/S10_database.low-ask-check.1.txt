internal knowledge

**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload outgrows it and you have numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four of your workloads acceptably, though none outstandingly. It gives you real transactions, and on orders those aren't negotiable. That settles which database holds the authoritative data.

How each option does on your workloads:

Postgres: real transactions for orders. Catalogue acceptable. Built-in text search good enough below a million documents, weak above. Analytics fine up to tens of millions of rows, after which scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. Nothing here favours it over Postgres.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. But multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, which is a poor fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search, with relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. Not an authoritative store, so it must be fed from another database, never replace it.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Where it usually ends up: Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **The real cost isn't the licence but the time of the people keeping each system up.**

When to add each one:

Elasticsearch when the catalogue passes a million documents or Postgres search quality is measurably failing. Below a million documents it's almost always premature.

ClickHouse when analytics passes tens of millions of rows and scans are measurably slow.
