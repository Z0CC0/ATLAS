Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene, which had already tried to create digital money with designs such as b-money and bit gold. None of them solved one key problem: how to order transactions without someone in charge.

In 2008 a paper titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" appeared, signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That name is a pseudonym, and the person behind it has never been identified. The paper set out to stop double spending, where the same money is spent twice, without a central body keeping the ledger.

Its solution was a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Each block takes real computing work to produce. To change an old block, an attacker would have to redo that work for the block and for every block after it, so rewriting the past becomes very expensive.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That headline proves the block was not made before that date, and it also signals what the project stood for.

New bitcoin come from the block reward. It started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, which is roughly every four years. Because of this, the total number of bitcoin approaches 21 million and never goes past it.

The first documented commercial transaction came in 2010, when someone paid 10,000 bitcoin for two pizzas.

Satoshi stopped taking part in public in 2011. The bitcoin Satoshi held have never been moved.
