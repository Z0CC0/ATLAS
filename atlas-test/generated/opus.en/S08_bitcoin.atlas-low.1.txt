Bitcoin started as a 2008 paper, "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System," signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That name is a pseudonym and has never been identified.

It grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts such as b-money and bit gold never solved ordering. That left double spending open: without a central body keeping the ledger, nothing stopped someone spending the same coin twice.

The paper's answer was a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Proof of work makes rewriting the past expensive, because the work would have to be redone for every later block.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It carries the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout, which works both as a timestamp and as a statement of intent.

The block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. Because of this, the total ever issued tends toward 21 million and goes no further.

The first documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin. In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part publicly, and Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
