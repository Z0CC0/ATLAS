**For a small project, use Postgres.** Switch to Redis later if your volume actually calls for it.

**Why Postgres fits a small project:**

- **It's fast enough.** A queue built on `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to about a thousand jobs per minute. That's well above what most small projects produce.
- **Jobs stay in sync with your data.** You enqueue the job in the same transaction as the write it depends on. If that transaction rolls back, the job goes with it, so a worker can never pick up a job for data that was never saved.
- **Your jobs survive crashes without extra setup.** If the process dies, the queue is still sitting in the database when you come back.
- **There's nothing new to run.** You already operate Postgres for your data.

**What Redis would cost you:**

- **One more service** to run, monitor and upgrade.
- **Persistence you have to set up yourself.** Without AOF enabled, a restart loses every job in the queue. That's an easy mistake to miss until it bites you.

**Where Redis wins:** it handles far more volume and has sub-millisecond latency. That matters once you're well past the thousand-jobs-per-minute range or need very fast pickup. A small project usually doesn't need either yet.

Start with Postgres and keep an eye on your job throughput. Move to Redis only if you start pushing against that limit.
