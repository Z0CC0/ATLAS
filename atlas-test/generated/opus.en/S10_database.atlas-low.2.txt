Start with **Postgres alone**. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when one workload outgrows it and you have numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. It gives real transactions, and on orders those aren't negotiable. Built-in text search works fine below a million documents and gets weak above that. Analytics holds up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

Alternatives, workload by workload:

Orders: MySQL matches Postgres here, but it's weaker on data types and complex queries, so it gains you nothing. MongoDB is risky for orders: multi-document transactions arrived late and they cost. ClickHouse doesn't fit, because point updates are its weak spot.

Catalogue: MongoDB makes this easier with variable schemas. That alone doesn't justify a second database next to the one holding orders.

Text search: Elasticsearch is far better, with relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. But it isn't an authoritative store. It has to be fed from another database, not replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

Analytics: ClickHouse is far better, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead.

When you outgrow Postgres, the usual setup keeps Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Each system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost isn't the licence but the time of the people keeping those systems running.

So the questions to watch are how many documents you'll search and how many rows the analytics will scan. Cross a million documents and search quality becomes the case for Elasticsearch. Pass tens of millions of rows with slow reports and that's the case for ClickHouse. Until then, Postgres covers all four.
