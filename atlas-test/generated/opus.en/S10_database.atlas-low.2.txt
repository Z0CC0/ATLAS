**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload proves it needs one, with numbers in hand.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. On orders it gives real transactions, and for orders those are not negotiable. Built-in text search works well enough below a million documents and turns weak above that. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, after which scans get slow.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but is weaker on data types and complex queries, so it gains you nothing here.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier thanks to variable schemas. But multi-document transactions arrived late and they cost, which is the wrong trade when orders are part of the platform.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. It is not an authoritative store, though. It has to be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. It is not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Where this usually ends up: Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Get there one step at a time, not on day one. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost of specialised systems is not the licence but **the time of the people keeping them up**.

In practice: launch on Postgres. Add Elasticsearch when the catalogue passes about a million documents or search quality measurably falls short. Add ClickHouse when analytics go past tens of millions of rows and scans get slow.
