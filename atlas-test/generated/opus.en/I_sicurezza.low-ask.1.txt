No. Anything that reaches the browser can be read, and obfuscation stops nobody: as soon as someone opens the developer tools or looks at the network requests, the key is right there.

Keep the key on the server. Have your frontend call an endpoint on your own backend, and let that backend call the service using the key.

Put a rate limit on that backend endpoint. Without one, anyone can use your endpoint as a free way to spend your key.

If the key has already been published, treat it as compromised and rotate it right away. Removing it from the code isn't enough.

Deleting it in a new commit doesn't help either. The key stays in the repository history, and anyone who already has a clone has a copy of it.
