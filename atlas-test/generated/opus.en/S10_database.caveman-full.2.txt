**Start with Postgres alone.** It covers all four workloads acceptably. Add a specialised system only when one workload outgrows Postgres and you have numbers to prove it.

**Postgres**
- Handles all four workloads acceptably. None of them outstandingly.
- Real transactions. For orders, you cannot do without them.
- Built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that.
- Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, scans get slow.

**MySQL**
- Equivalent to Postgres on orders.
- Weaker on data types and complex queries. It gains you nothing over Postgres here.

**MongoDB**
- Variable schemas make the catalogue easier.
- Multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly. That is a bad fit for orders, which need transactions.

**Elasticsearch**
- Far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos.
- It is not an authoritative store. It must be fed from another database, not replace it.
- Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

**ClickHouse**
- Far better at analytics. Its columnar scans are orders of magnitude faster.
- Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

**Target architecture if you grow:** Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. That is the usual setup.

**Price of each added system:**
- It brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem.
- The real cost is not the licence. It is the time of the people who keep it running.

**Path:**
1. Launch on Postgres for orders, catalogue, search and analytics.
2. Measure. Add Elasticsearch when search quality or volume demands it, well past a million documents.
3. Add ClickHouse when analytics scans get slow, past tens of millions of rows.
4. Keep Postgres as the authoritative store the whole time.
