**Recommendation: start with Postgres alone.** Add other systems only when a specific workload needs them, and only with numbers to prove it.

**Postgres**
- Handles all four workloads (orders, catalogue, text search, analytics) acceptably, but none of them outstandingly.
- Has real transactions. Orders can't do without them.
- Built-in text search is good enough below 1 million documents and weak above that.
- Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, scans get slow.

**MySQL**
- Same as Postgres on orders.
- Weaker on data types and complex queries. It gives you no reason to pick it over Postgres.

**MongoDB**
- Variable schemas make the catalogue easier.
- Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost performance. That makes it a poor fit for orders.

**Elasticsearch**
- Far better than Postgres at text search: relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance.
- It isn't an authoritative store. It has to be fed from another database and can't replace one.
- Below 1 million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

**ClickHouse**
- Far better than Postgres at analytics. Its columnar scans are orders of magnitude faster.
- It isn't for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

**How the usual setup grows**
Postgres stays the authoritative store. Elasticsearch and ClickHouse are fed from it when they're needed:
- Add Elasticsearch when search quality or volume outgrows Postgres, past roughly 1 million documents.
- Add ClickHouse when analytics outgrow Postgres, past tens of millions of rows and with slow scans.

**What each added system costs**
- Each new system adds a sync problem and a data alignment problem.
- The main cost isn't the licence. It's the time your people spend keeping the system running.
- So each new system has to earn its place with measured need.

Bottom line: use Postgres for orders and the catalogue now. Measure search and analytics as you grow. Split them out only when the numbers show you need to.
