internal knowledge

**Start with Postgres alone.** Add another system only when a specific workload demands it, with numbers in hand.

Postgres: handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. Real transactions, which are not negotiable on orders. Built-in text search good enough below a million documents, weak above. Analytics fine up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. Nothing gained over Postgres.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. Multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly, so it's a poor fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better text search, with relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store: it must be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

Each added system brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **Real cost is not the licence but the time of the people keeping it up.**

In practice:

1. Postgres for everything now.

2. Elasticsearch once the catalogue passes a million documents and search measurably falls short.

3. ClickHouse once analytics pass tens of millions of rows and scans slow down.
