Decide based on what the team must be able to do a year from now that it can't do today. That question isn't technical, and it should drive the architecture.

Before you choose, work out two things:

1. Which parts of the monolith actually change. The stable parts gain nothing from being split out.
2. Where the transaction boundaries sit. A transaction that spans two services becomes a distributed problem.

Avoid a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle the monolith progressively instead. Put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Microservices cost more to run. Every service needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always outweighs the benefit.

Consider a **modular monolith**, the option nobody mentions. It gives you sharp internal boundaries and a single release, and you can still extract a module the day you genuinely need to.
