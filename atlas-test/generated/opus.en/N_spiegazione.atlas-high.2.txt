Replicas don't agree right after a write, but they converge within a finite time once writes stop.

**Why you should care:** it hits whoever reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

The trade is against availability. A system demanding strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas can't talk to each other.

Convergence takes usually under a second across replicas in the same region, and rises to seconds across regions.

Common fix: send reads to the primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

It's a choice, not a defect to eliminate. Most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

Cases where it's unacceptable are few and recognisable: account balances, seat availability, uniqueness of an identifier.
