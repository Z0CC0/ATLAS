**Start with Postgres alone.** Add a specialised system only when a specific workload needs it, and you have the numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably and none of them outstandingly. Real transactions, which orders can't do without. Built-in text search, good enough below a million documents, weak above that. Analytics up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to pick it over Postgres.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. But multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly, which is a bad fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search, with relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. It isn't an authoritative store, so it must be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

The usual end state is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **The real cost isn't the licence but the time of the people keeping each system running.** So add Elasticsearch when search outgrows Postgres (around a million documents), and ClickHouse when analytics outgrows it (beyond tens of millions of rows). Don't add them before that.
