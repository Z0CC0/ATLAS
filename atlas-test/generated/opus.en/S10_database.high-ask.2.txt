Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload needs it and you have the numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably and none of them outstandingly. It gives real transactions, and on orders those are not negotiable. Its built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but is weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to pick it here.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier with variable schemas. Multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly, which hurts on orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. It is not an authoritative store, so it must be fed from another database and cannot replace it. Below a million documents it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster. It is not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

The usual end state is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data-alignment problem. The real cost is not the licence but the time of the people who keep it running.

**Decision rule:** move search to Elasticsearch once you pass about a million documents. Move analytics to ClickHouse once tens of millions of rows make scans slow. Orders stay in Postgres in every case.
