**Eventual consistency** means that right after a write, the copies of your data (replicas) don't all agree yet. If writes stop, they catch up with each other within a finite time.

## How long the gap lasts

- **Replicas in the same region:** under a second
- **Replicas in different regions:** a few seconds

## Why systems accept it

It's a trade against availability. A system that requires strong consistency has to refuse requests whenever its replicas can't talk to each other. A system that allows eventual consistency keeps answering.

So eventual consistency is a choice, not a defect you need to remove. Most systems that scale accept it on purpose.

## Why you should care

The person it affects is the one who reads right after they write:

1. A user posts a comment
2. They reload the page
3. Their comment isn't there, because the read went to a replica that hasn't caught up

To them, that looks like a bug.

**The usual fix:** send reads to the primary replica only for the session that just wrote. Everyone else keeps reading from the replicas, so you keep the scaling benefit.

## When it's not acceptable

There are only a few of these cases, and they're easy to spot:

- **Account balances**
- **Seat availability**
- **Unique identifiers**

In these cases, even a short disagreement between replicas causes real damage, like overdrafts, double-booked seats, or duplicate IDs. They need strong consistency.
