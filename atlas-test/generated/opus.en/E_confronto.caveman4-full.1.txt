Use Postgres. Move to Redis only when volume really demands it.

**Why Postgres fits a small project:**
- `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly 1,000 jobs per minute. Small projects rarely go past that.
- The job and its data go in the same transaction. If the write rolls back, the job is rolled back too, so it can never start on its own.
- Jobs persist with no extra setup. If the process dies, the queue is still there.
- You don't add a new service. Your existing DB does the job.

**What Redis gives you, and what it costs:**
- Much higher volume, and latency under a millisecond.
- It's one more service to run, monitor and upgrade.
- You have to set up persistence yourself. Without AOF enabled, a restart loses every queued job.

Switch to Redis when you actually need more than roughly 1,000 jobs per minute or latency under a millisecond. Until then, Postgres is simpler and safer.
