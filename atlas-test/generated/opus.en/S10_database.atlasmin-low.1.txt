Start with **Postgres alone**. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when one specific workload proves, with numbers, that Postgres no longer copes.

**Why Postgres:** it handles all four workloads acceptably, though none of them outstandingly. It gives real transactions, and on orders you can't do without them.

Text search: built in. Good enough below a million documents, weak above that.

Analytics: fine up to tens of millions of rows. Beyond that, scans get slow.

**Alternatives:**

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to prefer it here.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. But multi-document transactions arrived late and cost performance, which is a bad fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search, with relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. It can't be the authoritative store. It has to be fed from another database, never replace it. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

**Where you'll likely end up:** Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. That's the usual setup. Add each piece only when its workload demands it, not up front.

Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost of a specialised system isn't the licence. It's the time of the people keeping it running.
