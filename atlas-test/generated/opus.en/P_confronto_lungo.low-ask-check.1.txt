Neither alone: write to local files and run a collector that ships them elsewhere. At a thousand requests a minute, that way the application never waits on a log write.

Whatever you pick, one rule holds: **writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.**

Why not just files: zero cost, no dependency, but searching means grep, and rotation has to be configured by hand or the disk fills. Searching across several machines doesn't exist; you connect to each one.

Why not just managed service: instant search, alerts, automatic retention, but you pay by volume, and at a thousand requests a minute volume grows fast. Logs also leave your own infrastructure, so personal data has to be stripped before they go.

Why not application's database: searching is familiar and convenient, but logs and real requests are two very different workloads competing for the same database. Under load, logs fill caches and connections that real requests needed.

The collector is what lets you combine them. Files stay the cheap local write. The collector sends logs to wherever you search them, which can be a managed service if you accept its cost and strip personal data first.
