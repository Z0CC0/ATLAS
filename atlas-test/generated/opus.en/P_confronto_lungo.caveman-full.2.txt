Use local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere. At 1,000 requests a minute, that setup keeps the application from ever waiting on a log write.

One rule holds whatever you pick: writing a log must not sit in the request's critical path.

**Files alone**
- Zero cost, no dependency.
- Searching means grep.
- You configure rotation by hand. If you don't, the disk fills.
- No search across several machines. You connect to each one.

**Managed service**
- Instant search, alerts, automatic retention.
- You pay by volume. At 1,000 requests a minute, volume grows fast.
- Logs leave your own infrastructure. Strip personal data before they go.

**Database**
- Searching is familiar and convenient.
- In the application's own database, logs and real requests are two very different workloads, and they compete.
- Under load, logs fill caches and connections the real requests need.

**What to do**
1. Write logs to local files and configure rotation.
2. Run a collector next to the application. It ships the files to wherever you search: a managed service or a separate store.
3. If logs go to a managed service, strip personal data before shipping and watch the volume bill.

You get cheap local writes, search across all machines, and no log write blocking a request.
