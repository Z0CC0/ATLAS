**Local files plus a collector** that ships them elsewhere, so the application never waits on a log write.

Whatever you pick, writing the log must never sit in the request's critical path.

**Files alone:** zero cost, no dependency. But searching means grep, and you have to set up rotation by hand or the disk fills. You also can't search across several machines; you have to connect to each one.

**Managed service:** instant search, alerts and automatic retention. You pay by volume, though, and at a thousand requests a minute that volume grows fast. The logs also leave your infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they go.

**Database:** searching is familiar and convenient. Putting logs in the application's own database makes two very different workloads compete. Under load, logs fill the caches and connections that real requests needed.
