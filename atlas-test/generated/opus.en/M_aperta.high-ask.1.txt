Start from what team must do in a year that it cannot do today; that question, not technology, picks architecture.

1. **Which parts actually change.** Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

2. **Where transaction boundaries sit.** Transaction spanning two services becomes distributed problem.

Avoid full parallel rewrite: almost always fails. Old system keeps changing, copy never catches up.

Prefer progressive strangling instead. Router in front, move one piece at a time, old system stays authoritative until new piece verified.

Microservices cost more in operations: every service wants releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, and a person looking after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds benefit.

Consider modular monolith, the option nobody mentions: sharp internal boundaries, one release, and a module extracted only the day it is genuinely needed.
