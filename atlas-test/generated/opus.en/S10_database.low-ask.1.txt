**Start with Postgres alone.** Add other systems only when a specific workload demands it, and only with numbers in hand.

Postgres handles all four of your workloads acceptably, though none of them outstandingly. It gives real transactions, and on orders those aren't negotiable. Its built-in text search holds up below a million documents and gets weak above that. It carries analytics up to tens of millions of rows, after which scans get slow.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but is weaker on data types and complex queries, so it doesn't gain you anything here.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier because schemas can vary. But multi-document transactions arrived late and they cost, which counts against it for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It isn't an authoritative store, though. It has to be fed from another database, not replace one. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. It isn't for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Where you're likely to end up is the usual setup: Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Get there one system at a time, when search or analytics actually outgrows Postgres. Each system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost of specialised systems isn't the licence. It's the time of the people keeping them up.
