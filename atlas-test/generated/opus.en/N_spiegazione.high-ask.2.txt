Eventual consistency means replicas don't agree right after a write, but they converge within a finite time if writes stop.

It matters to whoever reads right after writing: a user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

The common fix is to read from the primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

Convergence is typically under a second within one region and rises to seconds across regions.

The trade is against availability: strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas can't talk to each other.

It's not a defect to eliminate but a choice. Most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

The cases where it's unacceptable are few and easy to spot: account balances, seat availability, and identifiers that must be unique.
