Eventual consistency means that after a write, replicas disagree for a while. If writes stop, they converge within a finite time.

It matters to whoever reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

Why systems accept it: availability. Strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas can't talk to each other.

The lag is short. Replicas in the same region usually converge in under a second. Across regions it rises to seconds.

Usual fix: send reads to the primary replica only for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

It isn't a defect to eliminate. It's a choice, and most systems that scale make it deliberately.

It's unacceptable in only a few cases, and they're easy to recognise: account balances, seat availability and identifier uniqueness.
