Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree immediately, but converge within finite time once writes stop.

Typical convergence: under a second between replicas in the same region, seconds across regions.

Why you should care: it hits whoever reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

Common fix: route reads to the primary replica only for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

Trade is against availability. A system demanding strong consistency must refuse requests when replicas can't talk to each other.

It's not a defect to eliminate but a choice. Most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

Unacceptable only in a few recognisable cases: account balances, seat availability, identifier uniqueness.
