Before choosing an architecture, find out which part of the monolith actually changes. Stable parts gain nothing from being split. Leave them alone.

Next, map where your transactions start and end. A transaction that ends up spanning two services becomes a distributed problem. So draw service lines around transactions, never through them.

**Don't rewrite everything alongside the old system.** A full parallel rewrite almost always fails. The old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

**Strangle it one piece at a time instead:**
1. Put a router in front of the monolith.
2. Move one piece to the new code.
3. Keep the old system as the source of truth until the new piece is verified.
4. Repeat with the next piece.

**Know what microservices cost to run.** Every service needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

**Consider a modular monolith.** Few people mention this option. You get sharp internal boundaries and a single release. You can still extract a module the day you genuinely need to.

**The deciding question isn't technical.** Ask what your team must be able to do a year from now that it can't do today. Choose the architecture that makes that possible, and nothing bigger.
