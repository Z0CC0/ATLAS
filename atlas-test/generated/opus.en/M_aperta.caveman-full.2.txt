Before choosing an architecture, answer these questions in this order.

**1. Which part changes?**
Map where change happens in the monolith. Only that part is worth restructuring. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

**2. Where are your transaction boundaries?**
Find which operations must commit together. A service boundary through a transaction turns it into a distributed problem. Draw boundaries around transactions, never through them.

**3. How will you migrate?**
Skip the parallel full rewrite. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up. Strangle the old system progressively instead:
- Put a router in front of the monolith.
- Move one piece at a time behind the router.
- Keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

**4. Can you afford microservices?**
Each service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, and one person who owns it. Below five developers, that operational cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

**5. Have you considered a modular monolith?**
Few people mention it, but it often fits best:
- sharp internal boundaries between modules
- one release
- you can extract a module into a service when you really need to, not before

**6. The deciding question is not technical.**
Ask what your team must be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today. Pick the architecture that makes that possible, and nothing beyond it.

If your team is small and the changing part is small, a modular monolith built by strangling the old system is probably the right start.
