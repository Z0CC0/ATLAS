**Local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere.** The app writes to local disk and never waits on a log write.

Rule that always holds: writing the log must not sit in the request's critical path.

**Why not files alone**
- Zero cost, no dependency.
- Search is only `grep`.
- Rotation has to be configured by hand, or the disk fills.
- No search across machines. You have to connect to each one.

**Why not a managed service alone**
- Instant search, alerts, automatic retention.
- You pay by volume. At 1000 req/min, volume grows fast.
- Logs leave your own infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they go.

**Why not the app's database**
- Search is familiar and convenient.
- Logs and real requests are very different workloads, and they compete.
- Under load, logs fill caches and connections the real requests needed.

The collector can ship to a managed service. If it does, the volume cost and stripping personal data still apply.
