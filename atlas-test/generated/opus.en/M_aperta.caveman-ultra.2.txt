Before choosing an architecture, answer these questions:

**1. Which parts change?**
Map which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from a split. Leave them alone.

**2. Where are the transaction boundaries?**
Find where each transaction starts and ends. A transaction that spans two services becomes a distributed problem. Draw service lines around transactions, never through them.

**3. Can you afford microservices?**
Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and one person who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

**4. Have you considered a modular monolith?**
Nobody mentions this option. You get sharp internal boundaries and one release. You can still extract a module on the day you genuinely need to. For a small team, this is often the right answer.

**5. How will you migrate?**
Don't run a full rewrite in parallel with the old system. That almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up. Strangle the old system progressively instead:
1. Put a router in front of the old system.
2. Move one piece at a time behind the router.
3. Keep the old system authoritative until you have verified the new piece.

**The deciding question isn't technical:** what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Choose the architecture that makes that possible. Everything else is secondary.
