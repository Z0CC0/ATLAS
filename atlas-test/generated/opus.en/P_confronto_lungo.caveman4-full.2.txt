Use local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere. At 1,000 requests/minute this is the sensible choice: the app writes locally and never waits on a log write. Whatever you pick, writing the log must never sit in the request's critical path.

**Files only**
- Zero cost, no dependency.
- Searching means grep.
- You configure rotation by hand. Skip it and the disk fills.
- No search across several machines. You connect to each one.

**Managed service**
- Instant search, alerts, automatic retention.
- You pay by volume. At 1,000 requests/minute, volume grows fast.
- Logs leave your own infrastructure. Strip personal data before they go.

**Database**
- Search is familiar and convenient.
- Logs in the app's own database put two very different workloads in competition.
- Under load, logs fill caches and connections that real requests needed.

Local files with a collector give you cheap local writes. Your search backend stays separate from the request path. If the collector ships to a managed service, strip personal data in the collector, before it sends.
