**Postgres**, until volume genuinely demands Redis.

`SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute.

Job and data live in the same transaction, so a job never starts while its matching write gets rolled back.

Persistence comes free: process dies, queue survives.

Redis handles far more volume, with latency under a millisecond. It's one more service to run, monitor and upgrade, though. Persistence also needs configuring: without AOF enabled, a restart loses queued jobs.
