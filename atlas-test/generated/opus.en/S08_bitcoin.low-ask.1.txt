Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene in 2008, with a paper signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym nobody has ever identified.

Earlier attempts like b-money and bit gold had tried to build digital money but never solved ordering, meaning how to agree on which transaction came first. Paper, titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System", went after that gap: double spending without any central body keeping the ledger.

Its answer: a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting the past gets expensive, because an attacker would have to redo the work for every later block too.

First block was generated on 3 January 2009. It carries the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout, which works both as a timestamp and as a statement of intent.

Supply was fixed from the start. Block reward began at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years, so total issuance tends to 21 million and never goes past it.

First documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part publicly. Their bitcoin have never been moved.
