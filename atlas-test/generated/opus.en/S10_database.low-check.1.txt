internal knowledge

Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload demands it and you have numbers to prove it.

Postgres: handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. Gives real transactions, which aren't negotiable on orders. Built-in text search good enough below a million documents, weak above. Carries analytics up to tens of millions of rows; past that, scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. Nothing here that favours it over Postgres.

MongoDB: makes catalogue easier with variable schemas, but multi-document transactions arrived late and cost. Wrong trade when orders depend on them.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store. It has to be fed from another database, not replace it. Below a million documents, adding it almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders: point updates its weak spot.

Where platforms like yours usually end up: Postgres as authoritative store, Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. But each added system brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. Real cost of specialised systems not the licence but the time of the people keeping them up. So grow into that setup one system at a time, only when search or analytics outgrows Postgres.
