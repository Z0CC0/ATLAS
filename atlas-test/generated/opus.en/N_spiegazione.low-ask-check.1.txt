internal knowledge

Eventual consistency means that after a write, replicas don't agree right away, but they converge within a finite time once writes stop.

It exists because of a trade against availability. A system that demands strong consistency has to refuse requests when its replicas can't talk to each other. Eventual consistency lets the system keep answering.

You should care because it affects whoever reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads the page, and doesn't see it.

The window is usually short. Across replicas in the same region, convergence typically takes under a second. Across regions, it rises to seconds.

The common fix is to read from the primary replica only for the session that just wrote, not for everyone. The writer sees their own comment, and everyone else keeps reading from the replicas.

Eventual consistency isn't a defect to eliminate. It's a choice, and most systems that scale make it on purpose.

The cases where it's unacceptable are few and easy to recognise: account balances, seat availability and uniqueness of an identifier. Those need strong consistency.
