internal knowledge

Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree right away, but converge within finite time once writes stop.

You should care because it hits whoever reads right after writing. User posts comment, reloads, doesn't see it.

Gap typically short: under a second between replicas in same region, a few seconds across regions.

Price of avoiding it: availability. System demanding strong consistency has to refuse requests whenever replicas can't talk to each other. So eventual consistency not a defect to eliminate but a choice, and most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

Common fix for the vanished comment: read from primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

Few cases where it's unacceptable, and easy to recognise: account balances, seat availability, uniqueness of an identifier.
