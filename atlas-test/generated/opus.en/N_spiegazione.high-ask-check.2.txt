internal knowledge

Eventual consistency means replicas don't agree right after a write. They converge within a finite time once writes stop.

Why you should care: a user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it. It bites anyone who reads right after writing.

Usual fix: read from the primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

Convergence usually takes under a second between replicas in the same region, and seconds across regions.

The trade is against availability. Strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas can't talk to each other.

So it's not a defect to eliminate. It's a choice, and most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

The cases where it's unacceptable are few and easy to recognise: account balances, seat availability and identifier uniqueness.
