internal knowledge

After a write, replicas don't agree right away. If writes stop, they converge within a finite time.

Why care: it hits anyone who reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

Common fix: the session that just wrote reads from the primary replica. Everyone else keeps reading from the other replicas.

Typical delay: under a second between replicas in the same region. Across regions it rises to seconds.

The trade is against availability. A system that demands strong consistency has to refuse requests when its replicas can't talk to each other.

**It's a choice, not a defect to eliminate.** Most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

**It's unacceptable in only a few cases, and they're easy to spot:** account balances, seat availability, and uniqueness of an identifier.
