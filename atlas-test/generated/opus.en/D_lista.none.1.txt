A static site has no logins or forms to protect, but browsers still need to be told how to treat it. These are the headers I'd set:

**Set these**

- **`Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains`** makes the browser always use HTTPS. Use a max-age of at least one year (31536000 seconds).
- **`Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'self'`** blocks injected third-party scripts. It's the most important header on the list. If you also add `frame-ancestors 'none'`, it protects against clickjacking.
- **`X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`** stops the browser from guessing a file's content type. It uses the type your server declares.
- **`Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`** sends other sites only your origin when someone follows a link out. Your full page paths stay private.
- **`X-Frame-Options: DENY`** also protects against clickjacking. It isn't needed if your CSP already has `frame-ancestors 'none'`, but sending both does no harm.
- **`Permissions-Policy: camera=(), microphone=(), geolocation=()`** turns off these features. A static site almost certainly doesn't use them.

**Leave this out**

- **`X-XSS-Protection`** is deprecated. Modern browsers ignore it, and in older browsers it actually introduced vulnerabilities. Don't send it.

**Example (nginx)**

```nginx
add_header Strict-Transport-Security "max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains" always;
add_header Content-Security-Policy "default-src 'self'; frame-ancestors 'none'" always;
add_header X-Content-Type-Options "nosniff" always;
add_header Referrer-Policy "strict-origin-when-cross-origin" always;
add_header X-Frame-Options "DENY" always;
add_header Permissions-Policy "camera=(), microphone=(), geolocation=()" always;
```

If your pages load anything from another domain, such as fonts, analytics or a CDN, `default-src 'self'` will block it. You'll need to add those domains to the CSP. Check your browser console for blocked requests after you deploy.
