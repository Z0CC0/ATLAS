Key question isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Let that drive the architecture.

Before deciding, find out:

**Which parts actually change.** Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

How many developers you have. Microservices cost more to run: every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person looking after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. You get sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.

How to migrate:

**Avoid a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system.** It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Use progressive strangling instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until the new piece is verified.
