Start with the question that isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Let that answer drive the architecture.

Find which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Find where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Don't do a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails: the old one keeps changing, and the copy never catches up.

Strangle progressively instead. Put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Microservices cost more in operations. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person looking after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider a **modular monolith**, the option nobody mentions: sharp internal boundaries, one release, and a module extracted only the day it's genuinely needed.
