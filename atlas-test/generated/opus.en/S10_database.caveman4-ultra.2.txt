**Pick: Postgres alone for now.** Add other systems only when a specific workload proves it needs one, with numbers in hand.

**Postgres**
- Handles all four workloads acceptably, none of them outstandingly.
- Real transactions. On orders these are not negotiable.
- Built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that.
- Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, scans get slow.

**MySQL**: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. Postgres beats it.

**MongoDB**: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. But multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, which is a bad trade when orders are in the same system.

**Elasticsearch**: far better at text search (relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance). It is not an authoritative store, so it has to be fed from another database, never replace one. Below a million documents it is almost always premature.

**ClickHouse**: far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

**Growth path**: the usual setup is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.
- Add Elasticsearch when the catalogue passes a million documents or search quality measurably falls short.
- Add ClickHouse when analytics passes tens of millions of rows and scans get slow.

**Cost of each added system**: one more synchronisation problem and one more data alignment problem. The real cost is not the licence. It is the time of the people keeping it running.
