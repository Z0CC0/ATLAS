**Start with Postgres alone.** It covers orders, catalogue, text search and analytics with a single database. It isn't outstanding at any of the four, but it is acceptable at all of them.

## Why Postgres
- **Orders:** it gives you real transactions, and on orders you can't do without them.
- **Text search:** it has search built in. That is good enough below about a million documents and weak above that.
- **Analytics:** it copes up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, the scans get slow.

## The other options

| Option | Where it wins | Where it loses |
|---|---|---|
| **MySQL** | Just as good as Postgres on orders | Weaker on data types and complex queries, so there's no reason to pick it over Postgres |
| **MongoDB** | Variable schemas make the catalogue easier | Transactions across several documents arrived late and are costly, which is risky for orders |
| **Elasticsearch** | Far better text search: relevance, synonyms, tolerance of typos | It can't be your main store. Another database has to feed it, so it sits alongside that database |
| **ClickHouse** | Far better analytics: its column-based scans are many times faster | Not for orders, because updating single rows is its weak spot |

## What the platform looks like as it grows
The usual setup is Postgres as the main store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Add those two only when you have numbers that show you need them:

1. **Add Elasticsearch** when search quality or catalogue size outgrows Postgres. Below a million documents it is almost always too early.
2. **Add ClickHouse** when analytics goes past tens of millions of rows and the scans slow down.

Each system you add brings two problems: keeping it in sync with Postgres, and keeping the data consistent between them. The licence isn't the real cost of a specialised system. The real cost is the time your people spend keeping it running.

Next: count the documents in your catalogue and the rows your analytics will scan. If they're under a million and under tens of millions, Postgres alone is enough.
