Postgres, and move to Redis only when your volume really needs it.

`SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute, which covers most small projects.

Jobs live in the same transaction as your data. If the write rolls back, the job never starts.

You get persistence for free: if the process dies, the queue is still there.

Redis has the edge on volume and latency. It handles far more jobs, at under a millisecond. But it's one more service to run, monitor and upgrade. Persistence also has to be configured: without AOF enabled, a restart loses the queued jobs.
