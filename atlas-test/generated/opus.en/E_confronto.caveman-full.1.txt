**Use Postgres.** Move to Redis only when job volume really needs it.

**Why Postgres fits small project:**
- `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to about a thousand jobs per minute. Small project rarely goes past that.
- Job and data live in same transaction. If write rolls back, job rolls back too. Job never starts for data that doesn't exist.
- Persistence comes free. If process dies, queue is still there.

**What Redis gives you, and what it costs:**
- Much more volume and lower latency, under one millisecond.
- One more service to run, monitor and upgrade.
- Persistence needs configuration. Without AOF enabled, restart loses queued jobs.

**When to switch:** move to Redis when Postgres can no longer keep up with job volume, or when you need sub-millisecond latency. Until then, Redis adds another service and more ways to lose jobs, and you get nothing back for it.
