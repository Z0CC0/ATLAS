Local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere. At a thousand requests a minute, that's the sensible setup, because the application never waits on a log write.

Whatever you pick, writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.

**Files alone** cost nothing and add no dependency. The downsides: searching means grep, and you have to set up rotation by hand or the disk fills. There's also no way to search across several machines; you'd have to connect to each one.

**A managed service** gives instant search, alerts and automatic retention. But you pay by volume, and at your traffic the volume grows fast. The logs also leave your infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they go.

**A database** makes searching familiar and convenient. But if you write logs into the application's own database, two very different workloads compete with each other. Under load, the logs fill caches and use up connections that the real requests needed.

The collector setup gets you the low cost of files without grep on every machine. Where it ships the logs is up to you: a managed service, as long as you strip personal data first, or a separate store.
