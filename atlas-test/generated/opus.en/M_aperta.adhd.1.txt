**Start here:** write one sentence answering *"What must the team be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today?"* That question decides the architecture, and the answer is not technical. Everything below checks your design against it.

## Before you choose an architecture

1. **Find which parts actually change.** List the areas of the monolith that change often and the ones that stay stable. Splitting out a stable part gets you nothing.
2. **Map the transaction boundaries.** Note where data has to be committed together. If one transaction ends up spanning two services, you now have a distributed problem to solve, so draw your boundaries around transactions, not across them.
3. **Count your developers.** With fewer than five, microservices almost always cost more to run than they give back. Every service needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, and a person who looks after it.

## How to do the rewrite

- **Don't do a full rewrite in parallel with the old system.** That approach almost always fails: the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.
- **Strangle it progressively instead:**
  1. Put a router in front of the monolith.
  2. Move one piece at a time behind the router.
  3. Keep the old system as the source of truth for each piece until the new version is verified.

## The option nobody mentions

**A modular monolith:** sharp internal boundaries between modules, but still one release. You can extract a module into its own service on the day you actually need to, and not before. With a small team, or with only a few parts that really change, this is often the right place to start.

**Next:** write the one-year sentence, then mark each area of the monolith as "changes often" or "stable." That takes about 10 minutes, and it tells you which pieces are worth separating, if any.
