internal knowledge

**Postgres**, for a small project.

With `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` it handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute, which is usually more than a small project needs.

Job lives in same transaction as data. If write rolls back, matching job never starts.

Persistence comes free: process dies, queue still there.

Redis wins on raw numbers: far more volume, latency under a millisecond. Cost is one more service to run, monitor and upgrade. Its persistence also needs configuring: without AOF enabled, a restart loses queued jobs.

Start on Postgres. Move to Redis only when volume genuinely demands it.
