**Pick: Postgres alone to start.** Add specialised systems later, only when a specific workload proves it needs one, with numbers in hand.

**Postgres**
- Handles all four workloads acceptably. None outstandingly.
- Real transactions. On orders these are not negotiable.
- Built-in text search is good enough below 1 million documents. Weak above that.
- Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, scans get slow.

**MySQL**
- Equivalent to Postgres on orders.
- Weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to prefer it here.

**MongoDB**
- Variable schemas make the catalogue easier.
- Multi-document transactions arrived late and carry a cost. Bad fit for orders.

**Elasticsearch**
- Far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance.
- Not an authoritative store. It must be fed from another database, never replace one.
- Below 1 million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

**ClickHouse**
- Far better at analytics. Columnar scans run orders of magnitude ahead.
- Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

**Target setup when you outgrow Postgres:** Postgres stays the authoritative store. Elasticsearch and ClickHouse are fed from it.

**Cost of adding each system:**
- Each one brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem.
- The real cost is not the licence. It is the time of the people keeping these systems running.

**Triggers to watch:**
- Catalogue search passes about 1 million documents, or relevance becomes a problem: add Elasticsearch.
- Analytics tables pass tens of millions of rows and scans slow down: add ClickHouse.
- Orders stay in Postgres at every stage.
