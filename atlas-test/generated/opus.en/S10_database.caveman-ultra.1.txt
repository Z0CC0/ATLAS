**Start with Postgres alone.** Add other systems only when a specific workload proves it needs them, with numbers in hand.

**Postgres**
- Covers all four workloads acceptably. None of them outstandingly.
- Gives real transactions. On orders these are not negotiable.
- Built-in text search is good enough below 1 million documents and weak above that.
- Carries analytics up to tens of millions of rows. After that, scans get slow.

**MySQL**
- Equivalent to Postgres on orders.
- Weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to pick it over Postgres here.

**MongoDB**
- Variable schemas make the catalogue easier.
- Multi-document transactions arrived late and they cost. Bad fit for orders, which is the part you can't get wrong.

**Elasticsearch**
- Far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, tolerance of typos.
- Not an authoritative store. It must be fed from another database, not replace one.
- Below 1 million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

**ClickHouse**
- Far better at analytics. Columnar scans are orders of magnitude ahead.
- Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

**Where this usually ends up**
- Postgres stays the authoritative store. Elasticsearch and ClickHouse are fed from it.
- Each added system brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem.
- The real cost is not the licence. It is the time of the people keeping those systems running.

**When to add what**
- Search quality falls short and the catalogue is past about 1 million documents: add Elasticsearch.
- Analytics reach tens of millions of rows and scans get slow: add ClickHouse.
- Neither is true yet: stay on Postgres.
