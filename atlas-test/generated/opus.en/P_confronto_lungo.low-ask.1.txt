**Local files, plus a collector that ships them elsewhere.** At a thousand requests a minute, that way the application never waits on a log write.

Whatever you pick, writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.

Files alone cost nothing and add no dependency. But searching means grep, and you have to set up rotation yourself or the disk fills. Across several machines there's no shared search, so you'd connect to each one in turn.

A managed service gives you instant search, alerts and automatic retention. You pay by volume, though, and at a thousand requests a minute that volume grows fast. Your logs also leave your own infrastructure, so you must strip personal data before they are sent.

A database makes searching familiar and convenient. The catch is that putting logs in the application's own database makes two very different workloads compete. Under load, the logs take up caches and connections that real requests needed.

Files plus a collector give you each option's strengths without its weak points. Writing to a local file is cheap and doesn't block the request. The collector moves logs off each machine, so you can search them in one place.
