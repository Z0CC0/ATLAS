internal knowledge

Bitcoin came out of cypherpunk scene, which had already tried digital money with projects like b-money and bit gold. Those attempts never solved one problem: ordering transactions.

In 2008 paper appeared titled *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*, signed Satoshi Nakamoto, pseudonym never identified. It tackled double spending, meaning same coin spent twice, without central body keeping ledger. Its answer: chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting past becomes expensive, because attacker would have to redo proof of work for every later block too.

First block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains headline of Times article about bank bailout. That reference works as timestamp, proving block could not have been made earlier, and as statement of intent.

Issuance follows fixed schedule. Block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years, so total issued tends to 21 million and no further.

First documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin. In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part publicly, and Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
