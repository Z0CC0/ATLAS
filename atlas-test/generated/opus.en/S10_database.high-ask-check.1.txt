internal knowledge

Start with Postgres alone. Add another system only when a specific workload needs it and you have numbers to show it.

**Postgres:** acceptable on all four workloads, outstanding on none. It has real transactions, which orders can't do without. Built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL: same as Postgres on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. You gain nothing over Postgres.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, so it's a poor fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better text search, with relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. It isn't an authoritative store, so it must be fed from another database, not replace it. Below a million documents it's almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Usual setup once you outgrow Postgres: Postgres stays the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

When to add each: Elasticsearch once search passes a million documents. ClickHouse once analytics pass tens of millions of rows and scans slow down.

**Cost of each addition:** every new system brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost isn't the licence but the time of the people keeping it running.
