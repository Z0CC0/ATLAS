internal knowledge

Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload needs it and you have numbers to show it.

Postgres: handles all four workloads acceptably, none of them outstandingly. Real transactions, which orders can't do without.

Text search: built into Postgres. Good enough below a million documents, weak above that.

Analytics: Postgres copes up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, scans get slow.

MySQL: as good as Postgres on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to pick it over Postgres.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost performance, so it's wrong for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search, with relevance ranking, synonyms and typo tolerance. It's not an authoritative store: it has to be fed from another database, never replace it. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

**Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem.** The real cost isn't the licence. It's the time of the people keeping each system running.
