No. Obfuscation does not protect the key. Anything that reaches the browser is readable. Anyone can open the developer tools or look at the network requests and see the key.

**What to do instead:**

1. Keep the key on the server.
2. Have the frontend call an endpoint on your own backend. That backend then calls the service with the key.
3. Put a rate limit on that backend endpoint. Without one, anyone can use your endpoint to call the service on your key for free.

**If the key has already been published**, in a deployed frontend or a pushed commit, treat it as compromised. Rotate it right away. Removing it from the code does not fix the leak. Even if you remove it from a commit, it stays in the repository history, and anyone who already has a clone has the key.
