Start with **Postgres alone**. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload demands it and you have numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four of your workloads acceptably, though none outstandingly. On orders it gives real transactions, which you can't do without there. Its built-in text search holds up below a million documents and gets weak above that. For analytics it copes up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but is weaker on data types and complex queries, so it gives you nothing Postgres doesn't.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier because it allows variable schemas. Its multi-document transactions arrived late and come at a cost, which counts against it for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It isn't an authoritative store, so it has to be fed from another database and can't replace one. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. It isn't for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

The usual setup is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost is **not the licence but the time of the people keeping it running**. That's why each extra system should wait until the numbers call for it.
