**Write logs to local files and run a collector that ships them somewhere else.** At a thousand requests a minute, this is the setup that keeps your app from ever waiting on a log write.

## The rule

Writing a log must never sit in the request's critical path, whichever storage you pick.

## How the three options compare

**Files only**
- Costs nothing and adds no dependency.
- Searching means `grep`.
- You have to configure rotation yourself, or the disk fills up.
- You can't search across several machines at once. You have to connect to each one.

**Managed service**
- Search is instant, and alerts and retention are handled for you.
- You pay by volume, and at a thousand requests a minute that volume grows fast.
- The logs leave your infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before sending them.

**Your application's database**
- Searching is familiar and convenient.
- Logs and real requests are very different workloads, and here they compete with each other.
- Under load, logs take up cache and connections that your real requests needed.

## Why files plus a collector wins

- The app writes to a local file, which is fast and never blocks.
- The collector moves the logs to wherever you search them. That fixes the files-only problem of logging into each machine separately.
- You keep the database's capacity for real requests.

## Steps

1. Configure log rotation on each machine so the disk can't fill.
2. Run a collector on each machine that ships the files to one central place.
3. If that central place is a managed service, strip personal data before the logs leave your infrastructure, and watch the volume because you pay for it.

Next: check whether log rotation is already configured on your API machines. That's step 1.
