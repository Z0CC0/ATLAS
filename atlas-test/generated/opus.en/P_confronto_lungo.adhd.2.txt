**Use local files plus a collector that ships them to central storage.** At 1,000 requests a minute that's the sensible setup, because the app writes to local disk and never waits on a log write.

## Next steps
1. Write logs to a local file on each machine.
2. Set up rotation now so the disk never fills.
3. Install a collector on each machine to ship the files to one central place.
4. Before the logs leave your infrastructure, strip personal data from them.

## Why not the other two on their own

**Files alone**
- Good: no cost, no dependency.
- Problems:
  - Searching means grep.
  - Rotation is a manual setup, and without it the disk fills.
  - You can't search across machines. You have to connect to each one.
- The collector fixes the search problem and keeps the good parts.

**Managed service**
- Good: instant search, alerts and automatic retention.
- Problems:
  - You pay by volume, and at 1,000 requests a minute the volume grows fast.
  - Logs leave your infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before sending them.
- It can still be where the collector sends the logs. Just watch the bill.

**Your app's own database**
- Good: searching is familiar and easy.
- Problems:
  - Logging and real requests are two very different workloads, and they compete for the same database.
  - Under load, log writes fill the caches and connections your real requests need.

## The rule that always applies
Writing a log must never sit in the request's critical path. Whatever you choose, a slow log destination should never slow down a request.

Next: check whether your app writes logs synchronously inside the request handler. If it does, fix that first.
