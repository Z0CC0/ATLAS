The deciding question isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Start there.

First, find which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Second, map the transaction boundaries. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Don't do a full parallel rewrite. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It has sharp internal boundaries and a single release, and you can extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
