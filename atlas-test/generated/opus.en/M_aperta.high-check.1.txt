internal knowledge

First: which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Second: where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Avoid a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old one keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. Keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and someone looking after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It gives sharp internal boundaries and a single release. You can still extract a module the day it is genuinely needed.

**The deciding question is not technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today?**
