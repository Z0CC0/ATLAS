**Local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere.** At a thousand requests a minute, that keeps the application from ever waiting on a log write.

Whatever you choose, writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.

Files alone cost nothing and add no dependency. Searching means grep, though. If you don't configure rotation by hand, the disk fills. Across several machines there's no single place to search, so you have to connect to each one.

A managed service gives you instant search, alerts and automatic retention. You pay by volume, and at your traffic the volume grows fast. Your logs also leave your own infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they go.

A database feels familiar and convenient to search. Putting logs in the application's own database, though, makes two very different workloads compete. Under load, the logs fill caches and use connections that the real requests needed.

The collector gives you the best of both. The application only appends to a local file, and the collector ships the lines in the background to whichever service or store you choose. If that destination is a managed service, the volume cost and personal-data stripping still apply.
