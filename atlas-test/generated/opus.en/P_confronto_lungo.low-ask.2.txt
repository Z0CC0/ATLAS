**Local files plus a collector** that ships them somewhere else. Application never waits on log write.

Whatever you pick, writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.

Why not the others alone:

**Files only:** zero cost, no dependency. But search means grep, and rotation must be configured by hand or disk fills. Across several machines there's no search at all; you connect to each one.

**Managed service:** instant search, alerts, automatic retention. But you pay by volume, and at a thousand requests a minute volume grows fast. Logs also leave your infrastructure, so personal data must be stripped before they go.

**Database:** search feels familiar and convenient. But logs in the application's own database put two very different workloads in competition. Under load, logs fill caches and connections that real requests needed.

Files plus a collector keeps the cheap, dependency-free write on the machine. The collector gets logs off the box, so you're not grepping each server. Where it ships them is a separate choice. If that's a managed service, the volume cost and personal-data stripping above still apply.
