Eventual consistency means that after a write, replicas don't agree right away. If writes stop, they converge within a finite time.

Why you should care: it affects whoever reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

The trade-off is with availability. A system that demands strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas can't talk to each other.

The window is usually short. Replicas in the same region typically converge in under a second. Across regions it rises to seconds.

The common fix is to read from the primary replica, but only for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

It isn't a defect to eliminate but a choice. Most systems that scale accept it on purpose.

The cases where it's unacceptable are few and easy to recognise: account balances, seat availability, and uniqueness of an identifier.
