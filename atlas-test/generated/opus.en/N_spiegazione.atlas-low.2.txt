Eventual consistency: after write, replicas don't agree right away, but they converge within finite time once writes stop.

You should care because it hits whoever reads right after writing. User posts comment, reloads, and doesn't see it. Their comment wasn't lost. The replica they read from just hasn't caught up yet.

How long the gap lasts depends on distance. Replicas in same region usually converge in under a second. Across regions it rises to seconds.

Why put up with it: consistency trades against availability. A system demanding strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas can't talk to each other. Eventual consistency keeps answering. So it's **not a defect to eliminate but a choice**, and most systems that scale make it deliberately.

Common fix for the reload problem: send reads to primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone. The writer sees their own change immediately, and everyone else keeps reading from replicas.

Where it's unacceptable: these cases are few and easy to recognise.

Account balances

Seat availability

Uniqueness of an identifier

Use strong consistency there. Elsewhere, eventual consistency is usually the right call.
