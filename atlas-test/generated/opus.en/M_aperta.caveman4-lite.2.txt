Start with the question that decides everything, and it is not technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today? Pick the architecture that makes that possible. Then work through these:

**1. Find what actually changes.** Map which parts of the monolith get frequent changes. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

**2. Map transaction boundaries.** If one transaction ends up spanning two services, you have a distributed problem. Draw service lines where transactions already end, not through the middle of one.

**3. Don't do a parallel full rewrite.** It almost always fails. The old system keeps changing, and the copy never catches up.

**4. Strangle it progressively instead:**
- Put a router in front of the monolith.
- Move one piece at a time behind it.
- Keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

**5. Count the operations cost of microservices.** Every service needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always outweighs the benefit.

**6. Consider a modular monolith.** It's the option nobody mentions. You get sharp internal boundaries and a single release. You can still extract a module into its own service on the day you genuinely need to.
