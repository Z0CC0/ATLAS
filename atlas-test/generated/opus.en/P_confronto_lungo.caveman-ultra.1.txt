Use local files plus a collector that ships them to a central place. At 1,000 requests a minute this keeps the application from ever waiting on a log write. That is the rule that always holds: writing a log must never sit in the critical path of the request.

**Files alone**
- Cost nothing and add no dependency.
- Searching means `grep`.
- You configure rotation by hand, or the disk fills.
- With several machines there is no shared search. You connect to each machine one by one.

**Managed service**
- Search is instant, and alerts and retention come built in.
- You pay by volume, and at 1,000 requests a minute the volume grows fast.
- Logs leave your own infrastructure, so you must strip personal data before shipping them.

**Database**
- Searching is familiar and convenient.
- Writing logs into the application's own database makes two very different workloads compete.
- Under load, logs fill the caches and connections that real requests need.

**Setup**
1. The application writes to local files, with rotation configured.
2. A collector runs separately and ships the files elsewhere.
3. That destination can be a managed service if you want its search and alerts. In that case, strip personal data before the logs leave.
