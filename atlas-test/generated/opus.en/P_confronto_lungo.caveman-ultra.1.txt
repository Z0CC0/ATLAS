**Use local files plus a collector.** The collector ships the files to a central place. The application never waits on a log write.

**Rule that always holds:** writing the log must not sit in the request's critical path. Judge each option by that rule first.

**Files only**
- Zero cost, no dependency.
- Search is `grep`.
- You must configure rotation by hand, or the disk fills.
- No search across several machines. You connect to each one.

**Managed service**
- Instant search, alerts, automatic retention.
- Billing is by volume. At 1,000 requests/minute, volume grows fast.
- Logs leave your infrastructure. Strip personal data before they ship.

**Database**
- Search is familiar and convenient.
- Logs in the application's own DB put two very different workloads in competition.
- Under load, logs fill caches and connections that real requests need.

**What to do:** write to local files with rotation configured. Run a collector that ships the files elsewhere. That destination can be a managed service, if its cost and the personal-data stripping work for you. The collector fixes the multi-machine search problem, and request latency stays independent of the log backend.
