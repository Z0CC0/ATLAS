Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree right away, but they converge within finite time once writes stop.

It's a trade against availability. A system demanding strong consistency has to refuse requests whenever replicas can't talk to each other. Eventual consistency keeps answering.

Why you should care: it bites whoever reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

Gap usually short. Replicas in the same region typically converge in under a second. Across regions it rises to seconds.

Common fix: route reads to the primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone. The writer sees their own comment, and everyone else keeps reading from nearby replicas.

**Not a defect to eliminate but a choice.** Most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

Cases where it's unacceptable are few and easy to recognise:

account balances

seat availability

uniqueness of an identifier

Outside those, design around the read-after-write moment and accept the rest.
