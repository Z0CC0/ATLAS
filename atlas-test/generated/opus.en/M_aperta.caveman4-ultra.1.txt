Before choosing an architecture, check these things:

**1. Which parts actually change.** Find the parts of the monolith that change often. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

**2. Where the transaction boundaries are.** If one transaction ends up spanning two services, it becomes a distributed problem. Draw your service lines so that doesn't happen.

**3. Avoid a full parallel rewrite.** Building a full copy alongside the old system almost always fails. The old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle the monolith gradually instead:
- Put a router in front of it.
- Move one piece at a time.
- Keep the old system authoritative until each new piece is verified.

**4. The operational cost of microservices.** Every service needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always outweighs the benefit.

**5. Consider a modular monolith.** Few people mention this option. You get sharp internal boundaries and a single release, and you can still extract a module on the day it's really needed.

**6. The deciding question isn't technical.** Ask what your team must be able to do a year from now that it can't do today. Pick the architecture that makes that possible.
