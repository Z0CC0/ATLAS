Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree right away, but they converge within finite time once writes stop. Same region, usually under a second; across regions, seconds.

Why care: it hits whoever reads right after writing. User posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

Usual fix: send reads to the primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone. That user sees their own comment, and everyone else still reads from fast replicas.

It's not a defect to eliminate but a choice. The trade is against availability: a system demanding strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas can't talk to each other. Most systems that scale accept eventual consistency deliberately.

Cases where it's unacceptable are few and easy to recognise: account balances, seat availability, uniqueness of an identifier. Use strong consistency there; elsewhere eventual consistency is usually the right call.
