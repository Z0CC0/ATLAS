The deciding question isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today? Pick the architecture that makes that possible.

Before that, two things to map in the monolith:

First, which parts actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Second, where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

**Don't do a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system.** It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up. Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until the new piece is verified.

On microservices: they cost more to operate. Every service needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, and someone who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

**Consider the modular monolith, the option nobody mentions.** It gives you sharp internal boundaries and one release. You can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
