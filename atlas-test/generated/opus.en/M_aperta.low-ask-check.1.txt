internal knowledge

Before choosing an architecture, settle one question: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? That question isn't technical, and the answers below follow from it.

Find out which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split off, so leave them alone.

Map where the transaction boundaries sit. A transaction that spans two services becomes a distributed problem, so draw service lines where transactions already end, not through the middle of them.

Count your developers. Microservices cost more to operate: every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always outweighs the benefit.

Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It gives you sharp internal boundaries and a single release, and you can still extract a module the day you genuinely need to.

**Don't do a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system.** It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

**Strangle it progressively instead.** Put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until each new piece is verified.
