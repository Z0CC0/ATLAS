**Write logs to local files and run a collector that ships them elsewhere.** At 1,000 requests a minute this is the sensible setup, because the application never waits on a log write.

Whatever you pick, writing a log line must never sit in the critical path of a request.

## Why not the other two on their own

**Files alone**
- Free and no dependencies.
- Searching means grep.
- You have to set up rotation by hand, or the disk fills.
- You can't search across several machines. You have to connect to each one.

**A managed service alone**
- Instant search, alerts and automatic retention.
- You pay by volume, and at 1,000 requests a minute the volume grows fast.
- Your logs leave your own infrastructure, so you must strip personal data before they're sent.

**Your application's database**
- Searching is familiar and convenient.
- Logging and serving requests are very different workloads, and they end up competing in the same database.
- Under load, logs take up cache and connections that real requests needed.

## How the recommended setup fixes this

- Files are a fast local write, so requests don't wait on logging.
- The collector removes the "connect to each machine" problem because it gathers logs from every machine into one place.
- If the collector sends to a managed service, the volume costs and the personal-data stripping still apply.

**Next step (about 2 minutes):** check whether log rotation is already set up on your servers. If it isn't, that's your first task, because the disk will fill.
