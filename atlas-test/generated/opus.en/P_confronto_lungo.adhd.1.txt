**Use local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere.** At a thousand requests a minute, your app writes to a local file and never waits on the log. A separate process moves the lines off the machine.

One rule applies whichever option you pick: **writing a log must never sit in the critical path of a request.**

## Why the other two lose at your volume

**Files alone**
- They cost nothing and depend on nothing.
- Search is `grep`.
- You have to set up rotation yourself, or the disk fills.
- You can't search across several machines at once. You have to log into each one.

**Managed service alone**
- You get instant search, alerts and automatic retention.
- You pay by volume, and at a thousand requests a minute the volume grows fast.
- Your logs leave your own infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they go.

**The application's own database**
- Search is familiar and convenient.
- Logs and real requests are two very different workloads, and they end up competing.
- Under load, logs fill the caches and connections your real requests needed. This is the worst of the three.

## The setup

1. The app writes logs to a local file, with log rotation set up so the disk can't fill.
2. A collector reads that file and ships the lines to one central place, so you can search all machines together.
3. If that central place is a managed service, remove personal data before shipping, and watch the bill as your traffic grows.

**Next:** check whether your app writes logs synchronously inside the request handler. If it does, fix that first.
