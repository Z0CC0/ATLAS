Bitcoin grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts such as b-money and bit gold did not solve transaction ordering.

In 2008 a paper appeared, "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System", signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That is a pseudonym, and the person behind it has never been identified.

The problem: double spending without a central body keeping the ledger.

The solution: a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting the past is expensive because the work must be redone for every later block.

3 January 2009: first block generated. It contains a Times headline about a bank bailout, which serves as both timestamp and statement of intent.

The block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. The total issued tends to 21 million, and no further.

2010: first documented commercial transaction, two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

2011: Satoshi stopped taking part publicly. Those bitcoin have never been moved.
