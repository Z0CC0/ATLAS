Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene, after earlier attempts like b-money and bit gold that never solved how to order transactions.

In 2008 a paper appeared: "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System", signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That name is a pseudonym and has never been identified.

The problem it tackled was double spending without a central body keeping the ledger.

Its solution was a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting the past is expensive, because the proof of work would have to be redone for every later block.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout, which works both as a timestamp and as a statement of intent.

The block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. Total issuance tends to 21 million and never goes past it.

The first documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011, and their bitcoin have never been moved.
