No. Obfuscation doesn't protect the key. Anything that reaches the browser can be read. Someone only has to open the developer tools or look at the network requests, and the obfuscated key is exposed.

**What to do instead:**

1. Keep the key on the server.
2. Have the frontend call an endpoint on your own backend. That endpoint calls the service with the key.
3. Put a rate limit on that endpoint. Without one, anyone can use your endpoint as a free way to use your key.

**If the key was already published:**

Treat it as compromised and rotate it now. Removing it from the code is not enough. Taking it out in a later commit doesn't help either, because the key stays in the repository history. Anyone who already has a clone also has the key.
