Start with one question: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? The answer decides the architecture, and it isn't a technical question. The technical checks come after it.

1. Find which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

2. Find where the transaction boundaries sit. A transaction that spans two services becomes a distributed problem, so draw service lines where transactions already end.

Don't run a full rewrite in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

**Strangle it progressively instead.** Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Count the operational cost of microservices before choosing them. Every service needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

**Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions.** It gives you sharp internal boundaries and a single release. You can still extract a module the day you genuinely need to.
