internal knowledge

Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree right away, but if writes stop, they converge within finite time.

**Why care:** it bites whoever reads right after writing. You post a comment, reload, and it's gone. Not lost, just served by a replica that hasn't caught up yet.

How long that window lasts: under a second between replicas in same region, seconds across regions.

Why accept it at all: trade against availability. A system demanding strong consistency must refuse requests whenever replicas can't talk to each other. Eventual consistency keeps answering instead.

So it's not a defect to eliminate but a choice, and most systems that scale make it deliberately.

Usual fix for the vanishing-comment problem: route reads to primary replica only for the session that just wrote, not for everyone. Writer sees their own change immediately; everyone else still reads from replicas.

**Where it's unacceptable**: few cases, easy to spot.

Account balances

Seat availability

Uniqueness of an identifier

If your data falls in one of those, you need strong consistency there. Anywhere else, eventual consistency is probably fine.
