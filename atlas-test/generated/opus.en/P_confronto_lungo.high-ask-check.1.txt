internal knowledge

Use local files with a collector that ships them elsewhere. At 1,000 requests a minute this is the sensible choice: the application never waits on a log write.

The rule that always holds: writing the log must not sit in the request's critical path.

**Files alone** cost nothing and add no dependency. Searching means grep, though, and you have to set up rotation by hand or the disk fills. You also can't search across several machines at once; you have to connect to each one.

**A managed service** gives you instant search, alerts and automatic retention. You pay by volume, and at 1,000 requests a minute the volume grows fast. Your logs also leave your own infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they're sent.

A database makes searching familiar and convenient. Writing logs into the application's own database puts two very different workloads in competition, though. Under load, the logs fill caches and use connections that the real requests needed.
