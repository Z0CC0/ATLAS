**Postgres**, until volume genuinely calls for Redis.

Why it fits small project:

`SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute.

Job lives in same transaction as data, so job never starts while matching write gets rolled back.

Persistence comes free: if process dies, queue still there.

What Redis buys, and costs:

Far more volume, lower latency, under a millisecond.

One more service to run, monitor and upgrade.

Persistence needs configuring: without AOF enabled, restart loses queued jobs.

Move to Redis once job volume outgrows Postgres.
