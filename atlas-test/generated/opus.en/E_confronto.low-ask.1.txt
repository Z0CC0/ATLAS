**Postgres**, until volume genuinely demands Redis.

With `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, Postgres handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute. That covers most small projects.

Jobs live in the same transaction as your data. If the matching write rolls back, the job never starts.

You get persistence for free. If the process dies, the queue survives.

Redis handles far more volume, with latency under a millisecond. But it's one more service to run, monitor and upgrade. Its persistence also has to be configured: without AOF enabled, a restart loses the queued jobs.

Move to Redis when you outgrow those thousand jobs per minute or need sub-millisecond latency. Until then, Postgres is simpler and safer.
