internal knowledge

Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree right away, but they converge within a finite time once writes stop.

Why care: it hits whoever reads right after writing. User posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

How long the gap lasts: usually under a second across replicas in the same region, rising to seconds across regions.

Why systems accept it: the trade is against availability. A system demanding strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas cannot talk to each other. Most systems that scale accept eventual consistency deliberately. It's a choice, not a defect to eliminate.

Common fix for the reload problem: read from the primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone. The writer sees their own comment, and everyone else keeps reading from replicas.

Where it's unacceptable: few cases, and easy to recognise.
