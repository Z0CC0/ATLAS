At a thousand requests a minute, write logs to local files and run a collector that ships them elsewhere. The application writes to disk and moves on. The collector forwards the logs in the background, so a request never waits on a log write.

One rule holds whatever you pick: **writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.**

**Files only**
- Zero cost and no dependency.
- Searching means grep.
- You must configure rotation by hand, or the disk fills.
- You can't search across several machines. You have to connect to each one.

**Managed service**
- You get instant search, alerts and automatic retention.
- You pay by volume, and at a thousand requests a minute the volume grows fast.
- The logs leave your own infrastructure, so you must strip personal data before they go.

**Database**
- Searching is familiar and convenient.
- Writing logs into the application's own database puts two very different workloads in competition.
- Under load, the logs fill caches and connections that the real requests needed. For your traffic, this is the option to avoid.

**What to do**
- Write to local files and set up rotation.
- Run a collector that ships the files to a central place, so you can search across machines.
- If that central place is a managed service, watch the volume bill and strip personal data before shipping.
