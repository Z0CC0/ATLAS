At a thousand requests a minute, write logs to **local files and run a collector that ships them elsewhere**. The application only appends to a local file and moves on. The collector sends the logs to wherever you search them, so a request never waits on a log write.

One rule holds whatever you choose: **writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.**

Here is how the three options compare for your load.

**Files alone**
- They cost nothing and add no dependency.
- Searching means `grep`.
- You have to set up rotation by hand, or the disk fills.
- You can't search across several machines at once. You have to connect to each one.

**Managed service**
- Search is instant, and you get alerts and automatic retention.
- You pay by volume, and at a thousand requests a minute the volume grows fast.
- Your logs leave your own infrastructure, so you must strip personal data before they're sent.

**Your application's database**
- Searching is familiar and convenient.
- Logs and real requests are very different workloads, and here they compete with each other.
- Under load, logs take up cache and connections that your real requests needed.

The recommended setup keeps what's good about files: they're cheap and the write is fast. The collector covers what files lack, which is search across machines. You can still point the collector at a managed service later. If you do, you'll pay by volume and you'll need to strip personal data first. Keep logs out of the application database.
