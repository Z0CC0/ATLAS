**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload needs it, and only once you have numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably and none of them outstandingly. It gives real transactions, which you can't do without on orders. Built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, and past that the scans get slow.

MySQL is equivalent on orders. It's weaker on data types and complex queries, so it gives you no reason to prefer it.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier with variable schemas. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, which counts against it for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It isn't an authoritative store, so it has to be fed from another database, not replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. It isn't for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

The usual end state is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost isn't the licence. It's the time of the people keeping those systems up.

In practice: add Elasticsearch when the catalogue passes about a million documents or search quality becomes a measured problem. Add ClickHouse when analytics pass tens of millions of rows and the scans get slow.
