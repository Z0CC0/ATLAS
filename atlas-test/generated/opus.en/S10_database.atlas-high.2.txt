**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when one workload demands it, with numbers in hand.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, none of them outstandingly. Real transactions, not negotiable on orders. Built-in text search good enough below a million documents, weak above. Analytics fine up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to prefer it.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier, but multi-document transactions arrived late and cost. Bad fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search, with relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store. Must be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres as authoritative store, Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every added system brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **Real cost of specialised systems is not the licence but the time of the people keeping them up.**
