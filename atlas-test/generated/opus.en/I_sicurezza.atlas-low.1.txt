No. Obfuscation doesn't protect a key in the frontend. Anything that reaches the browser can be read, and the obfuscation falls apart as soon as someone opens the developer tools or looks at the network requests.

Keep the key on the server. Your frontend calls an endpoint on your own backend, and that backend calls the service with the key.

Put a rate limit on that backend endpoint. Without one, anyone can use it as a free way to spend your key.

If the key has already been published, treat it as compromised and rotate it immediately. Removing it from the code isn't enough.

Taking it out of a commit doesn't fix the leak either. The key stays in the repository history, and anyone who already has a clone has it.
