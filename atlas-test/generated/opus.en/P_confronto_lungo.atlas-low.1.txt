At a thousand requests a minute, write logs to local files and run a collector that ships them elsewhere. The application never waits on a log write. Whatever setup you pick, writing a log must never sit in the request's critical path.

**Files alone**: zero cost and no dependency. Searching means grep, and you have to configure rotation yourself or the disk fills. With several machines there's no way to search them together, so you connect to each one.

**Managed service**: instant search, alerts and automatic retention. You pay by volume, though, and at your traffic the volume grows fast. Logs also leave your own infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they go.

**Database**: searching is familiar and convenient. But writing logs into the application's own database makes two very different workloads compete. Under load, logs fill the caches and use up connections that real requests needed.

Files plus a collector gives you the cheap local write of the first option and search in one place. That place can be a managed service, if you strip personal data before shipping.
