**Local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere.** At 1000 req/min, the app never waits on a log write.

**Rule that always holds:** writing the log must not sit in the request's critical path.

**Files alone:**
- Zero cost, no dependency.
- Searching means grep.
- Configure rotation by hand, or the disk fills.
- No search across several machines. You have to connect to each one.

**Managed service:**
- Instant search, alerts, automatic retention.
- You pay by volume, and at 1000 req/min the volume grows fast.
- Logs leave your own infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they go.

**Application's own database:** avoid it.
- Searching is familiar and convenient.
- Log writes and real requests are two very different workloads, and they compete.
- Under load, logs fill the caches and connections your real requests needed.

The collector can feed a managed service. In that case, strip personal data at the collector and keep an eye on volume cost.
