internal knowledge

Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload outgrows it and you have numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably but none outstandingly. On orders it gives you real transactions, and there those are not negotiable. Its built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. It carries analytics up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, scans get slow.

MySQL is equivalent on orders but weaker on data types and complex queries, so it offers nothing here that Postgres doesn't.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier with variable schemas. But multi-document transactions arrived late and cost you, which is the wrong trade for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search, with better relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. It is not an authoritative store, though. It has to be fed from another database and cannot replace it. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. It is not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

The usual end state is **Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it**. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **The real cost is not the licence but the time of the people keeping it running.** So add each system only when its workload demands it.
