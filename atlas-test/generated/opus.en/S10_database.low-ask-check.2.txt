internal knowledge

**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when one specific workload proves Postgres too slow, and you have numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four of your workloads acceptably, though none of them outstandingly. Orders get real transactions, and on orders you can't do without them. Built-in text search holds up below a million documents and gets weak above that. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but is weaker on data types and complex queries. Nothing in your four workloads favours it.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier because it handles variable schemas. But multi-document transactions arrived late and carry a cost, which is a poor fit when orders need them without exception.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It isn't an authoritative store, though. It has to be fed from another database, not replace one. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. It isn't for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

If you outgrow Postgres, the usual setup keeps Postgres as the authoritative store and feeds Elasticsearch and ClickHouse from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost isn't the licence. **It's the time of the people keeping those systems running.**

Your next step is to measure your search corpus size and analytics row counts. Those two numbers tell you if and when to add each specialised system.
