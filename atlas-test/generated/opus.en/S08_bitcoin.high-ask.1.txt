Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts, b-money and bit gold, never solved how to put transactions in order.

In 2008 a paper titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" appeared, signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That is a pseudonym, and the person behind it has never been identified. The paper tackled double spending without a central body keeping the ledger. Its solution was a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting the past is expensive because the proof of work would have to be redone for every later block.

On 3 January 2009 the first block was generated. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout, which serves as both a timestamp and a statement of intent.

The block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. The total issued tends to 21 million and goes no further.

In 2010 came the first documented commercial transaction: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part publicly. Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
