Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts such as b-money and bit gold did not solve how to order transactions.

2008: a paper titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" appears, signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That is a pseudonym, never identified.

Problem it tackles: double spending, without a central body keeping the ledger.

Solution: a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting the past is expensive, because the work would have to be redone for every later block.

3 January 2009: the first block is generated. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout, which works both as a timestamp and as a statement of intent.

Issuance: the block reward starts at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. The total issued tends to 21 million and goes no further.

2010: the first documented commercial transaction, two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

2011: Satoshi stops taking part publicly. Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
