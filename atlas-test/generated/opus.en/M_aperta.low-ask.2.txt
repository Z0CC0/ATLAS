Start from what team must be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today. That question decides architecture, not technology.

Before choosing, map two things in monolith:

Which parts actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

Where transaction boundaries sit. Transaction spanning two services becomes distributed problem, so draw service lines where transactions already end, never through them.

Then check team size against operational cost. Every microservice wants its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and person who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds benefit.

Consider **modular monolith**, option nobody mentions: sharp internal boundaries, one release, and freedom to extract module the day it's genuinely needed. Often gives you most of what microservices promise without operational bill.

On how to migrate: avoid full rewrite running in parallel with old system. It almost always fails, because old one keeps changing and copy never catches up. Use **progressive strangling** instead:

1. Put router in front of monolith.

2. Move one piece at a time, starting with parts that change.

3. Keep old system authoritative until new piece is verified.
