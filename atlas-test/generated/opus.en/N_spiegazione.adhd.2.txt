**Short version:** after a write, your replicas disagree for a moment, and then they catch up. Most of the time that's fine. It becomes a problem when the user who just wrote something reads it back right away.

## What it is

When you write data, the replicas don't all get the update at the same instant. If writes stop, they **converge within a finite time**. Until then, different replicas can return different answers.

How long "a moment" lasts:
- **Same region:** usually under a second
- **Across regions:** a few seconds

## Why systems accept it

It's a trade against **availability**. A system that demands strong consistency has to **refuse requests** when its replicas can't talk to each other. Eventual consistency lets the system keep answering.

So it's not a defect you need to remove. It's a choice, and most systems that scale make it on purpose.

## Where it hurts

The person affected is whoever **reads right after writing**:

1. User posts a comment
2. User reloads the page
3. The read goes to a replica that hasn't caught up, so the comment isn't there

Everyone else reading a second later never notices.

## The usual fix

For the **session that just wrote**, send reads to the **primary replica**. Don't do this for everyone. Everyone else keeps reading from replicas, so you keep the scaling benefit and fix the one case users actually see.

## When it's not acceptable

There are only a few cases, and they're easy to spot:
- **Account balances**
- **Seat availability**
- **Uniqueness of an identifier** (e.g. no two users with the same username)

If your data isn't like these, eventual consistency is very likely the right default.
