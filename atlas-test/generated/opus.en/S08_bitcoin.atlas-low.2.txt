Bitcoin started with a 2008 paper, *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*, signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That name is a pseudonym, and no one has ever identified the person behind it.

The idea came out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts at digital money, such as b-money and bit gold, had come close but never solved one key problem: how to put transactions in an agreed order. Without that order, the same coin can be spent twice. The usual fix for this double spending is a central body that keeps the ledger. The paper aimed to do without one.

Its answer was a chain of blocks, ordered by proof of work. Each block takes real computing effort to produce. To rewrite a past block, an attacker would have to redo the work for that block and for every block after it, so changing history quickly becomes too expensive.

The network went live on 3 January 2009 with the first block. It contained the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That headline proved the block could not have been made before that date, and it also showed what the project stood against.

New bitcoin enter circulation as a reward for each block. The reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. Because of this, the total supply approaches 21 million and never goes beyond it.

The first documented purchase came in 2010, when someone paid 10,000 bitcoin for two pizzas. In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part publicly, and the bitcoin Satoshi held have never been moved.
