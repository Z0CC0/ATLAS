For a small project, use **Postgres**. Move to Redis later, when your volume actually needs it.

**Why Postgres fits:**
- **It handles your load.** `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` is enough for up to about a thousand jobs per minute. Most small projects stay well under that.
- **Jobs and data commit together.** The job is written in the same transaction as the data it depends on. If that write rolls back, the job rolls back too, so a job never starts for data that doesn't exist.
- **The queue survives crashes.** Persistence comes for free. If your process dies, the queued jobs are still in the table.

**What Redis gives you, and what it costs:**
- Redis handles far more volume, with latency under a millisecond.
- It is one more service to run, monitor and upgrade.
- You have to configure persistence yourself. Without AOF enabled, a restart loses every queued job.

At small scale you don't need Redis's speed, but you would still pay its costs. Start with Postgres. Switch when job volume starts pushing past what `SKIP LOCKED` handles.
