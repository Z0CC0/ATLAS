For a small project, use **Postgres**. Move to Redis only when your volume actually requires it.

**Why Postgres fits:**
- **Throughput is enough.** `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute. Most small projects stay well below that.
- **Jobs stay in step with your data.** You enqueue the job in the same transaction as the data write. If the write rolls back, the job rolls back too, so a job never runs against data that doesn't exist.
- **Persistence needs no setup.** If the process dies, the queue is still in the database.
- **You run nothing new.** You already operate Postgres.

**What Redis offers:**
- It handles far more volume, with latency under a millisecond.
- It is one more service to run, monitor and upgrade.
- You must configure persistence yourself. Without AOF enabled, a restart loses the queued jobs.

**When to switch:** Move to Redis when you get close to the thousand-jobs-per-minute range, or when you need sub-millisecond latency. Until then, Redis adds operational work and data-loss risk without a real benefit.
