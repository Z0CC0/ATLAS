Bitcoin came out of a 2008 paper, **Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System**, signed by Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym never identified.

Roots in cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts such as b-money and bit gold got close but never solved ordering: agreeing which transaction came first.

Core problem the paper tackles: double spending, meaning the same money spent twice, without a central body keeping the ledger. Satoshi's answer: a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Each block costs computation, so rewriting the past gets expensive. Anyone changing an old block would have to redo the work for every later block too.

First block generated on 3 January 2009. It carries the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That reference works both as a timestamp and as a statement of intent.

Issuance fixed from start. Block reward begins at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. Total supply therefore tends toward 21 million and never goes beyond.

First documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part publicly. Their bitcoin have never been moved.
