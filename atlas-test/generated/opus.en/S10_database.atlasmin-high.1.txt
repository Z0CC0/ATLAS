**Postgres alone** to start. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload needs it and you have numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. It gives real transactions, which orders can't do without. Its built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but is weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to pick it here.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier with variable schemas. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost performance, which is a bad fit for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. It is not an authoritative store, so it must be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster. It is not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

Each system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The **real cost** is not the licence but the time of the people keeping it running.
