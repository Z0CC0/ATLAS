**Start with Postgres alone.** Add other systems only when a specific workload needs them, and you can show it with numbers.

Postgres covers all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. It has real transactions, and on orders those aren't negotiable. Its built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. It handles analytics up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to choose it over Postgres here.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, which is a bad fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search, with relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. It's not an authoritative store, so it has to be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

If you outgrow Postgres, the usual setup keeps it as the authoritative store and feeds Elasticsearch and ClickHouse from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **The real cost isn't the licence but the time of the people keeping each system running.**
