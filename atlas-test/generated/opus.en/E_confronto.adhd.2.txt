**Use Postgres.** Switch to Redis only when your job volume actually requires it.

**Why Postgres fits a small project:**
1. **Throughput is enough.** `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to about 1,000 jobs per minute.
2. **Jobs share a transaction with your data.** If the write rolls back, the job rolls back with it. A job can never start for data that was never saved.
3. **Persistence is built in.** If the process dies, the queue is still there.
4. **No extra service.** You already run Postgres.

**What Redis offers, and what it costs you:**
- It handles far more volume, with latency under 1 ms.
- It's one more service to run, monitor and upgrade.
- You have to configure persistence yourself. If AOF isn't enabled, a restart loses every queued job.

**When to switch:** once you're regularly near 1,000 jobs per minute, or you need sub-millisecond latency.
