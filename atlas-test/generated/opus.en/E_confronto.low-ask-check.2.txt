**Postgres**, for a small project. Move to Redis only when volume genuinely demands it.

Postgres with `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute, enough for most small projects.

Job sits in same transaction as data, so it can never start while its matching write gets rolled back.

Persistence comes free: if the process dies, the queue survives.

Redis handles far more volume, with latency under a millisecond, but it's one more service to run, monitor and upgrade.

With Redis, persistence has to be configured. Without AOF enabled, a restart loses queued jobs.
