Start with one question, and it isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today? Every other choice should follow from the answer.

**Before you pick an architecture, map two things:**

1. **Which parts of the monolith actually change.** Stable parts gain nothing from being split. Leave them alone.
2. **Where the transaction boundaries sit.** If one transaction spans two services, it becomes a distributed problem. Draw your service lines so that doesn't happen.

**Don't do a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system.** This almost always fails. The old system keeps changing, and the copy never catches up.

**Strangle it progressively instead:**
- Put a router in front of the monolith.
- Move one piece at a time behind it.
- Keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

**Count the operational cost of microservices honestly.** Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

**Consider the modular monolith.** It's the option nobody mentions:
- sharp internal boundaries
- one release
- you can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed

If your team is small, or you can't yet point to which parts change, start with the modular monolith. Extract services only where your one-year goal needs it.
