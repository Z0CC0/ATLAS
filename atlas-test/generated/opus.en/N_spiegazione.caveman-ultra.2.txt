**Eventual consistency:** after a write, replicas disagree for a while. Once writes stop, replicas converge within finite time.

**How long:** same region, usually under one second. Across regions, it rises to seconds.

**Why it exists:** you trade it against availability. Strong consistency forces the system to refuse requests when replicas cannot talk to each other. Eventual consistency keeps serving them.

**Why you should care:** the problem hits whoever reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and the comment is missing. Users see that as a bug.

**Common fix:** the session that just wrote reads from the primary replica. Everyone else keeps reading from the other replicas. Primary load stays low.

**Mindset:** eventual consistency is not a defect to eliminate. It is a choice. Most systems that scale accept it on purpose.

**Where you cannot accept it:** these cases are few and easy to recognise:
- account balances
- seat availability
- identifier uniqueness

These need strong consistency. Everything else can usually tolerate a second or two of lag.
