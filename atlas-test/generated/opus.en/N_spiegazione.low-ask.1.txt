Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree right away, but they converge within a finite time once writes stop.

Why care: it hits whoever reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and the comment isn't there.

How long the gap lasts: usually under a second between replicas in the same region, rising to seconds across regions.

Why systems accept it: the alternative costs availability. A system that demands strong consistency has to refuse requests when its replicas can't talk to each other. So eventual consistency is a choice, not a defect to eliminate, and most systems that scale make that choice on purpose.

Common fix for the reload problem: send reads to the primary replica only for the session that just wrote, not for everyone. The writer sees their own comment, and everyone else keeps reading from the replicas.

Where it's unacceptable: only a few cases, and they're easy to recognise. These are account balances, seat availability and the uniqueness of an identifier. For those, pay for strong consistency.
