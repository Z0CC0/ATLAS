Use local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere. At 1,000 requests a minute, the app then never waits on a log write.

One rule always holds: writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request. Here is how each option fares:

**Files alone.** Zero cost and no dependency. But searching means grep. You must configure rotation by hand, or the disk fills. Across several machines there is no search at all: you connect to each one.

**Managed service.** You get instant search, alerts and automatic retention. But you pay by volume, and at your traffic the volume grows fast. Logs also leave your infrastructure, so strip personal data before they go.

**Database.** Searching is familiar and convenient. But logs in the application's own database compete with requests, and the two workloads are very different. Under load, logs fill the caches and connections that real requests needed.

Files plus a collector covers the gaps. The app writes locally and fast. The collector moves logs to a searchable place, which can be a managed service or a separate store. If the destination is outside your infrastructure, strip personal data before shipping. Configure rotation on the local files either way.
