**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload demands it and you have numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, but none of them outstandingly. On orders it gives you real transactions, which you can't do without. Built-in text search holds up below a million documents and gets weak above that. Analytics run fine up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but is weaker on data types and complex queries. Nothing here favours it.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier because it allows variable schemas. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost performance, which is a bad trade when orders are the part you can't get wrong.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It can't be your main database, though. It has to be fed from another database, not replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics. Its columnar scans are orders of magnitude faster. It's not for orders, because updating single records is its weak spot.

If you outgrow Postgres, the usual setup keeps Postgres as the main database (the source of truth) and feeds Elasticsearch and ClickHouse from it. Each added system brings two problems: keeping it in sync, and keeping its data consistent with Postgres. The real cost of these specialised systems isn't the licence. It's the time your people spend keeping them running.
