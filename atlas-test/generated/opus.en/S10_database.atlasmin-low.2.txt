Start with Postgres alone. Add other systems only when a specific workload proves it needs them, with numbers in hand.

Postgres handles all four of your workloads acceptably, though none of them outstandingly. It gives real transactions, and on orders those aren't negotiable. Its built-in text search holds up below a million documents and gets weak above that. Analytics run fine up to tens of millions of rows, after which scans get slow.

How the alternatives compare:

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but falls behind on data types and complex queries. It has no advantage here.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier with variable schemas. But multi-document transactions arrived late and come at a cost, which hurts on orders.

Elasticsearch does text search far better: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. It isn't an authoritative store, though. It has to be fed from another database, not replace one. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse does analytics far better, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Point updates are its weak spot, so it isn't suitable for orders.

The usual setup when you outgrow Postgres keeps Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **The real cost of specialised systems isn't the licence but the time of the people keeping them running**, and that should weigh on any decision to add one.

In practice: run Postgres. Add Elasticsearch when the catalogue passes a million documents and search quality measurably suffers. Add ClickHouse when analytics passes tens of millions of rows and scans get too slow.
