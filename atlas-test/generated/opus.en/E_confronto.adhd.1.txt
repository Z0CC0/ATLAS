**Use Postgres.** Switch to Redis only when your job volume actually needs it.

**Why Postgres fits a small project**
- **It's fast enough.** With `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` it handles up to about 1,000 jobs per minute.
- **Jobs and data stay consistent.** The job is written in the same transaction as your data. If the write rolls back, the job never runs.
- **The queue survives crashes by default.** If the process dies, the queued jobs are still in the database.
- **No new service.** You already run Postgres.

**What Redis costs you**
- **One more service** to run, monitor and upgrade.
- **Persistence isn't on by default.** Without AOF enabled, a restart loses every queued job.

**When Redis wins**
- It handles far more volume than Postgres.
- Its latency is under a millisecond.

**When to switch:** if you get close to about 1,000 jobs per minute, or you need sub-millisecond pickup, move to Redis and enable AOF.
