**Recommendation:** start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse later, only when one workload outgrows Postgres and you have numbers that show it.

## The options

**Postgres** handles all four of your workloads acceptably, but none of them outstandingly. It is the right base for these reasons:
- **Orders:** it gives real transactions, and on orders you cannot negotiate on them.
- **Text search:** it is built in. It is good enough below a million documents and weak above that.
- **Analytics:** it carries up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, the scans get slow.

**MySQL** is as good as Postgres on orders. It is weaker on data types and complex queries, so it gives you nothing Postgres doesn't.

**MongoDB** makes the catalogue easier because it allows variable schemas. The problem is orders: multi-document transactions arrived late and they cost, and orders are where you need transactions most.

**Elasticsearch** is far better than Postgres at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It is not an authoritative store. You feed it from another database. You can't use it to replace one.

**ClickHouse** is far better than Postgres at analytics, because its columnar scans are orders of magnitude faster. It is not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

## How the pieces fit

The usual setup is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. That is where you end up if search and analytics grow. It is not where you start. Each system you add creates a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem that you then have to manage.

## When to add a system

- **Elasticsearch:** add it when your search volume passes a million documents, or when you can measure that search quality is not good enough. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.
- **ClickHouse:** add it when your analytics tables reach tens of millions of rows and the scans become measurably slow.

## The real cost

The licence is not the main cost of a specialised system. The main cost is the time of the people who keep it running. Before you add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse, check that the gain is worth that ongoing work.
