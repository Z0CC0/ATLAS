Use local files plus a collector that ships them somewhere else. At a thousand requests a minute, this means the application never waits on a log write.

Whatever you choose, follow one rule: writing the log must never be in the critical path of the request.

**Why not files alone:**
- They cost nothing and add no dependency.
- Searching means grep.
- You have to set up rotation by hand, or the disk fills.
- You can't search across several machines at once. You have to connect to each one.

**Why not a managed service alone:**
- You get instant search, alerts and automatic retention.
- You pay by volume, and at a thousand requests a minute the volume grows fast.
- Your logs leave your own infrastructure, so you must strip personal data before sending them.

**Why not the application's database:**
- Searching is familiar and convenient.
- Logs and real requests are very different workloads, and they compete for the same database.
- Under load, logs fill the caches and connections your real requests need.

With files plus a collector, you keep the fast, cheap local write. The collector then gives you central search somewhere else. If that destination is a managed service, set up rotation on the local files and strip personal data before the collector ships them.
