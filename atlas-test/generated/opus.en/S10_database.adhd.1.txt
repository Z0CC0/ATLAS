**Start with Postgres alone.** Add another system only when a specific workload proves, with numbers, that Postgres can't keep up.

## Why Postgres first

- It handles all four of your workloads acceptably, though none of them outstandingly.
- It gives you real transactions. For orders, you can't do without them.
- Every extra system you add brings two problems: keeping the systems in sync and keeping the data aligned between them.
- The real cost of specialised systems isn't the licence. It's the time of the people who keep them running.

## Each workload

| Workload | Postgres alone | When to add something | What to add |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Orders** | Real transactions, the right tool | Never | Nothing. ClickHouse is weak at point updates, and MongoDB's multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly |
| **Catalogue** | Acceptable | Only if variable schemas become a real pain | MongoDB makes this easier, but you'd give up transactions on orders |
| **Text search** | Built-in, fine below 1M documents | Above 1M documents, or when you need better relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance | Elasticsearch, fed from Postgres. It can't be your main store. Adding it below 1M documents is almost always premature |
| **Analytics** | Fine up to tens of millions of rows | When scans get slow past that point | ClickHouse, fed from Postgres. Its columnar scans are orders of magnitude faster |

## Options you can drop

- **MySQL:** equal to Postgres on orders, but weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to choose it.
- **MongoDB as the main database:** it only wins on the catalogue, and it's weak where you can't afford to be, which is orders.

## Where you'll probably end up

The usual mature setup is **Postgres as the main store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it**. Get there one system at a time, only when a workload forces the move.

**Next:** count your catalogue documents and your analytics rows. Below 1M documents and tens of millions of rows, Postgres alone is the answer for now.
