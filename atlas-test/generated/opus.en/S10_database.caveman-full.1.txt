Start with Postgres alone. Add other systems only when one workload proves, with numbers, that Postgres can't cope.

**Per workload**

- **Orders:** Postgres. Real transactions, and on orders you can't give those up. MySQL is equal here, but weaker on data types and complex queries. MongoDB's multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly. ClickHouse is ruled out because point updates are its weak spot.
- **Catalogue:** MongoDB makes it easier with variable schemas. That alone doesn't justify a second database next to the orders one. Postgres handles the catalogue acceptably.
- **Text search:** Postgres has built-in search. It's good enough below a million documents and weak above. Elasticsearch is far better on relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. Adding Elasticsearch below a million documents is almost always premature.
- **Analytics:** Postgres copes up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, scans get slow. ClickHouse is far better there, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster.

**The usual setup at scale**

Postgres stays the authoritative store. Elasticsearch and ClickHouse sit beside it and are fed from it. Elasticsearch never replaces Postgres, because it isn't an authoritative store.

**The trade-off**

Postgres covers all four workloads acceptably and none of them outstandingly. Each system you add fixes one workload but brings a sync problem and a data-alignment problem. The real cost isn't the licence. It's the time of the people who keep each system running.

**What to do**

1. Launch on Postgres alone.
2. Measure document count and table sizes over time.
3. Add Elasticsearch once search passes a million documents and relevance starts to suffer.
4. Add ClickHouse once analytics passes tens of millions of rows and scans get slow.
5. Skip MySQL, because it adds nothing over Postgres here. Skip MongoDB, because easier catalogue schemas aren't worth weaker transactions on orders.
