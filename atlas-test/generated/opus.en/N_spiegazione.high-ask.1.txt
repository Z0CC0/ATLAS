Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas disagree for a while, then converge within finite time once writes stop.

Why it exists: trade against availability. Strong consistency must refuse requests when replicas cannot talk.

Why you care: user posts comment, reloads, does not see it. Hits whoever reads right after writing.

Common fix: read from primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

How long: under a second across replicas in same region; seconds across regions.

Not a defect to eliminate but a choice. Most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

Unacceptable only in few, recognisable cases: account balances, seat availability, uniqueness of an identifier.
