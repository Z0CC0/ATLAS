**Pick: Postgres alone to start.** Add other systems later, only when one workload proves it needs them with real numbers.

**Postgres**
- Handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly.
- Real transactions. For orders, transactions are not negotiable.
- Built-in text search is good enough below 1 million documents and weak above.
- Carries analytics up to tens of millions of rows. After that, scans get slow.

**MySQL**
- Equal to Postgres on orders.
- Weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to choose it over Postgres here.

**MongoDB**
- Variable schemas make the catalogue easier.
- Multi-document transactions arrived late and have a cost. That is a bad fit for orders.

**Elasticsearch**
- Far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, tolerance of typos.
- Not an authoritative store. It must be fed from another database, not replace one.
- Below 1 million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

**ClickHouse**
- Far better at analytics. Columnar scans are orders of magnitude ahead.
- Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

**Usual end state:** Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

**Cost of each extra system:**
- Each one adds a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem.
- The real cost is not the licence. It is the time of the people who keep the system running.

**Path:**
1. Launch on Postgres only.
2. Search weak and catalogue past 1 million documents? Add Elasticsearch.
3. Analytics past tens of millions of rows with slow scans? Add ClickHouse.
4. Orders stay in Postgres permanently.
