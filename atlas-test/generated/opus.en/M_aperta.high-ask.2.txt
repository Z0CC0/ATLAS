Two questions decide it: which parts of your monolith actually change, and what your team must be able to do a year from now that it can't today. The second is not a technical question.

**Before choosing**

Find which parts actually change. Splitting out stable parts gains nothing.

Map where the transaction boundaries sit. A transaction split across two services becomes a distributed problem.

**Rewrite strategy**

Avoid a full rewrite built in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

**Architecture**

Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and someone who looks after it.

With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always outweighs the benefit.

A modular monolith is the option people tend to overlook. It has sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can pull a module out the day you genuinely need to.
