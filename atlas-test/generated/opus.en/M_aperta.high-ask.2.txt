Start with what the team must be able to do a year from now that it can't do today. That question isn't technical, and it decides the architecture.

Find which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Map where the transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Count the operational cost. Every microservice needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. Below five developers that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider the modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It gives sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.

**Avoid a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system.** It almost always fails: the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

**Strangle it progressively instead.** Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. Keep the old system authoritative until the new one is verified.
