**Use Postgres.** For a small project it's the better choice. Move to Redis only when your volume truly needs it.

**Why Postgres fits:**
- `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to about a thousand jobs per minute. Most small projects stay well under that.
- The job is written in the same transaction as your data. If that write rolls back, the job rolls back too, so a job can never start on data that doesn't exist.
- Persistence comes free. If the process dies, the queue is still there.
- You don't add anything new to your stack.

**What Redis costs you:**
- It's one more service to run, monitor and upgrade.
- You have to configure persistence yourself. Without AOF enabled, a restart loses the queued jobs.

**What Redis gives you:**
- It handles far more volume, with latency under a millisecond. That matters only once Postgres can't keep up.

Start on Postgres. Switch when you get near the limit of about 1,000 jobs per minute, or when you need sub-millisecond latency.
