Use local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere. At 1,000 requests/minute, this setup means the app never waits on a log write.

The rule that always holds: writing the log must not sit in the request's critical path.

**Files alone**
- Zero cost, no dependency.
- Search is grep only.
- You have to set up rotation by hand, or the disk fills.
- No search across machines. You have to connect to each one.

**Managed service**
- Instant search, alerts, automatic retention.
- You pay by volume. At 1,000 requests/minute, volume grows fast.
- Logs leave your own infrastructure. Strip personal data before they go.

**Database**
- Search is familiar and convenient.
- Logs in the app's own database put two very different workloads in competition.
- Under load, logs fill the caches and connections that real requests needed.

The collector can ship to a managed service. If it does, the volume cost and the personal-data stripping still apply.
