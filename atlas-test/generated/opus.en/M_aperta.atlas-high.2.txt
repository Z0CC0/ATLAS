Start with the question that isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Choose the architecture that answers it.

**Before choosing:**

First, which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Second, where the transaction boundaries sit. A transaction that spans two services becomes a distributed problem.

**On team size:** microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. Below five developers that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

The option nobody mentions is the modular monolith. It has sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.

On the migration itself, avoid a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead:

1. Put a router in front of the old system.
2. Move one piece at a time.
3. Keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.
