Deciding question isn't technical: what must team be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today? Everything else serves that answer.

First: which parts of monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Second: where transaction boundaries sit. Transaction spanning two services becomes distributed problem.

Don't run full rewrite in parallel with old system. Almost always fails: old one keeps changing, copy never catches up.

Strangle progressively instead. Put router in front, move one piece at a time, keep old system authoritative until new piece verified.

Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and person looking after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds benefit.

Consider modular monolith, option nobody mentions. Sharp internal boundaries, one release, and you can extract module the day it's genuinely needed.
