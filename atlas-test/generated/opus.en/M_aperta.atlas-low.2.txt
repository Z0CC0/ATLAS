Before choosing, find out which parts of your monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

Next, map where your transaction boundaries sit. Once a transaction spans two services, it turns into a distributed problem.

Don't do a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. That almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Count what microservices cost to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Look at the modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. You get sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can still extract a module the day you genuinely need to.

**The deciding question isn't technical:** what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Pick the architecture that answers it.
