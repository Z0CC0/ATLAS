internal knowledge

Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene, after earlier attempts such as b-money and bit gold that never solved ordering.

In 2008 a paper titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" appeared, signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym never identified. It tackled double spending without a central body keeping the ledger. Its solution: a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Proof of work makes rewriting the past expensive, because it would have to be redone for every later block.

First block generated 3 January 2009, carrying the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That reference worked both as a timestamp and as a statement of intent.

Block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly four years, so total issued tends to 21 million and no further.

First documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid with 10,000 bitcoin. Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011, and Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
