For a small project, use **Postgres**. Move to Redis only when your job volume actually requires it.

**Why Postgres fits:**
- `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly 1,000 jobs per minute. That is plenty for a small project.
- The job lives in the same transaction as your data. If the write rolls back, the job rolls back with it, so a job can never start for data that doesn't exist.
- The queue is persistent with no extra setup. If the process dies, the jobs are still there.
- You run no extra service.

**What Redis offers, and what it costs:**
- It handles far more volume, with latency under a millisecond.
- It is one more service to run, monitor and upgrade.
- You have to configure persistence yourself. If AOF is not enabled, a restart loses the queued jobs.

Start with a jobs table in Postgres. Switch to Redis when you're well past about 1,000 jobs per minute or you need sub-millisecond latency.
