internal knowledge

Bitcoin grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts at digital money, such as b-money and bit gold, never solved ordering: agreeing which transaction came first.

In 2008 a paper appeared, **Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System**, signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That pseudonym has never been identified. The paper tackled double spending, meaning spending the same coin twice, without any central body keeping the ledger.

Its answer: a chain of blocks, ordered by proof of work. Rewriting the past gets expensive, because an attacker would have to redo the proof of work for every later block.

The first block came on 3 January 2009. It carries the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That reference works both as a timestamp and as a statement of intent.

The rules for issuing new coins were set from the start. The block reward began at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. So the total supply approaches 21 million and never goes past it.

The first documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part publicly. Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
