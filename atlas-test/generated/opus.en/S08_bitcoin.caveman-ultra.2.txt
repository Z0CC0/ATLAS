Bitcoin grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier designs such as b-money and bit gold tried to create digital cash. Neither solved ordering, meaning agreement on which transaction came first.

**The paper.** In 2008 a paper appeared: *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*. It was signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym that has never been identified. The paper tackled double spending, where the same coin is spent twice, without a central body keeping the ledger.

**The solution.** Transactions are grouped into a chain of blocks, ordered by proof of work. Changing an old block means redoing the proof of work for that block and for every block after it. That makes rewriting the past expensive.

**The launch.** The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. The headline works two ways:
- as a timestamp, since the block cannot be older than that day's paper
- as a statement of intent, showing money outside the banking system

**Issuance.** The block reward started at 50 bitcoin. It halves every 210,000 blocks, which is roughly every four years. Total supply therefore approaches 21 million and never goes past it.

**First real use.** The first documented commercial transaction was in 2010: two pizzas bought for 10,000 bitcoin.

**Satoshi's exit.** Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011. Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
