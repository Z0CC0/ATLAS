Start with what your team must be able to do a year from now that it can't do today. That question decides the architecture, and it isn't a technical one. Everything below serves it.

**First, find which parts of the monolith actually change.** Stable parts gain nothing from being split. Leave them alone.

**Second, map where transactions sit.** If one transaction spans two services, it becomes a distributed problem. Draw your boundaries where transactions already end.

Don't rebuild it all in parallel. A full rewrite running beside the old system almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until each new piece is verified.

Count what microservices cost to run. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and someone who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It has sharp internal boundaries and a single release. You can still extract a module the day you really need to.
