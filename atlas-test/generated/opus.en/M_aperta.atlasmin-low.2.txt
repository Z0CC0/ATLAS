Start with what team must be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today. Question isn't technical, and its answer decides the rest.

Then look at monolith itself:

**Which parts actually change.** Stable parts gain nothing from being split; leave them where they are.

**Where transaction boundaries sit.** Transaction spanning two services becomes distributed problem, so draw service lines along those boundaries, not across them.

How to migrate matters as much as target. Full rewrite running in parallel with old system almost always fails: old one keeps changing and copy never catches up. Strangle it progressively instead: put router in front, move one piece at a time, keep old system authoritative until new piece is verified.

Before choosing microservices, count operational cost. Every service wants its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, and person who looks after it. Below five developers that cost almost always exceeds benefit.

Option nobody mentions: modular monolith. Sharp internal boundaries, one release, and freedom to extract module day it's genuinely needed. For small team, or one unsure where boundaries really are, often right starting point.
