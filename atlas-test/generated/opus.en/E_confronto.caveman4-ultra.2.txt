**Postgres.** Switch to Redis only when volume really needs it.

**Why Postgres:**
- `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly 1,000 jobs/min.
- The job is written in the same transaction as the data. If the write rolls back, the job is rolled back too, so it never runs.
- Persistence comes free. If the process dies, the queue is still there.

**Redis costs:**
- One more service to run, monitor and upgrade.
- Persistence has to be configured. Without AOF enabled, a restart loses the queued jobs.

**Redis advantage:** far more volume, and latency under 1 ms. That only matters past roughly 1,000 jobs/min, which is when moving to Redis makes sense.
