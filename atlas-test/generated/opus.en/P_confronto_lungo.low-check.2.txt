internal knowledge

Use local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere. That way your API never waits on a log write.

Files alone cost nothing and need no dependency. But searching means grep, and you have to set up rotation by hand or the disk fills. Once you run several machines, there's no way to search across them; you have to connect to each one.

A managed service gives you instant search, alerts and automatic retention. You pay by volume, though, and at a thousand requests a minute the volume grows fast. Your logs also leave your own infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they go.

A database makes searching familiar and convenient. But putting logs in the application's own database makes two very different workloads compete. Under load, the logs fill caches and use up connections that the real requests needed.

Whatever you choose, one rule always holds: **writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.**
