**Start with Postgres alone.** Add another system only when a specific workload demands it and you have the numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. Real transactions, which orders can't do without. Built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL is equivalent on orders. It's weaker on data types and complex queries.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier with variable schemas. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, a problem for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. It's not an authoritative store, so it has to be fed from another database, not replace it. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. It's not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

When to add them: Elasticsearch when search grows past a million documents. ClickHouse when analytics grow past tens of millions of rows.

Every added system brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **The real cost isn't the licence but the time of the people keeping them up.**
