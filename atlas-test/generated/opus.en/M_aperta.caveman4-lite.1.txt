Start with one question: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today? That question decides the architecture, and it is not a technical one. Everything below feeds into it.

**Map what changes.** Find which parts of the monolith actually get modified. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

**Map transaction boundaries.** Find where your transactions begin and end. A transaction that spans two services becomes a distributed problem. Draw service lines where transactions already separate, not through them.

**Don't do a parallel full rewrite.** It almost always fails. The old system keeps changing while you build, and the copy never catches up.

**Strangle progressively instead:**
1. Put a router in front of the monolith.
2. Move one piece at a time behind it.
3. Keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

**Count the operational cost of microservices.** Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

**Consider a modular monolith.** It is the option nobody mentions. You get sharp internal boundaries and one release. You can still extract a module the day it is genuinely needed.

So the decision runs in this order:
1. Answer the one-year question.
2. Map what changes and where transactions sit.
3. Check team size against operational cost.
4. Pick between a modular monolith and services.
5. Migrate by strangling the old system piece by piece, never by rewriting it all at once.
