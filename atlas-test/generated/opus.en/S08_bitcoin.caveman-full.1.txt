Bitcoin came from cypherpunk circles. Earlier designs such as b-money and bit gold tried to create digital money. None of them solved how to agree on the order of transactions.

In 2008 a paper appeared: *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*. It was signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym. Nobody has ever found out who used it.

The paper tackled double spending. Digital money is only data, so the same coin could be spent twice. Normally a central body keeps the ledger and blocks this. Bitcoin removed that central body.

Its answer was a chain of blocks, put in order by proof of work. Each block costs real computing effort to make. To change an old block, an attacker must redo the work for that block and for every block after it. So rewriting history is very expensive, and the ledger stays trustworthy without anyone in charge.

The first block was created on 3 January 2009. It contains a Times headline about a bank bailout. The headline does two jobs. It proves the block was not made before that date. It also states the project's purpose: money that does not depend on banks.

The supply rules were fixed from the start. The block reward began at 50 bitcoin. It halves every 210,000 blocks, which is about every four years. So the total supply approaches 21 million and never goes higher.

In 2010 came the first documented purchase: two pizzas, paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part in public. Satoshi's bitcoin have never moved since.
