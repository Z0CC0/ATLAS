Bitcoin grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier designs such as b-money and bit gold tried to build digital cash. None solved ordering: deciding which transaction came first without someone in charge.

**Paper (2008).** Paper appears titled *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*. Signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym. Real author never identified.

**Problem.** Double spending. Digital money is easy to copy, so the same coin could be spent twice. Usual fix is a central body keeping the ledger. Paper wants no such body.

**Solution.** Chain of blocks, ordered by proof of work. Each block needs costly computation. Rewriting a past block means redoing proof of work for that block and every later block. So the past gets more expensive to change as the chain grows.

**Genesis block (3 January 2009).** First block contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That reference does two jobs. It is a timestamp: block cannot predate that headline. It is also a statement of intent: money outside the bank-bailout system.

**Issuance rules.** Block reward starts at 50 bitcoin. Reward halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. Total issued tends toward 21 million and no further.

**First real use (2010).** First documented commercial transaction: two pizzas, paid with 10,000 bitcoin.

**Creator exits (2011).** Satoshi stops public participation. Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
