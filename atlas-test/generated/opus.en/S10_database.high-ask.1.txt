**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only once a specific workload demands it, with numbers in hand.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. It has real transactions, and on orders those aren't negotiable. Built-in text search good enough below a million documents, weak above. Analytics fine up to tens of millions of rows; past that, scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. Nothing gained over Postgres.

MongoDB: catalogue easier with variable schemas, but multi-document transactions arrived late and cost. That's a problem for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search (relevance, synonyms, tolerance of typos). Not an authoritative store; must be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, since point updates are its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres as authoritative store, Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Each added system brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **Real cost isn't the licence, it's the time of the people keeping them up.**

Signals for adding a system: search past a million documents or needing relevance and typo tolerance points to Elasticsearch. Analytics past tens of millions of rows with slow scans points to ClickHouse.
