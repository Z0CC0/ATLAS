**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload needs it, and only with numbers to back that up.

**Postgres:** handles all four workloads acceptably but none of them outstandingly. It gives real transactions, which you can't do without on orders. Its built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. It carries analytics up to tens of millions of rows; past that, scans get slow.

**MySQL:** equivalent on orders, but weaker on data types and complex queries. It has nothing Postgres lacks here.

**MongoDB:** variable schemas make the catalogue easier. But multi-document transactions arrived late and cost performance, which makes it a poor fit for orders.

**Elasticsearch:** far better at text search, with relevance ranking, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It isn't an authoritative store, so it has to be fed from another database and can't replace it. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

**ClickHouse:** far better at analytics, because columnar scans are orders of magnitude faster. It isn't for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

**Where platforms usually end up:** Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Each system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost isn't the licence. It's the time of the people keeping those systems running.
