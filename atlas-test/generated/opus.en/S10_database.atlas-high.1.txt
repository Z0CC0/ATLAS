Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload demands it, and only once you have numbers to show it.

**Postgres**: handles orders, catalogue, text search and analytics acceptably, none of them outstandingly. Real transactions, which orders cannot do without. Built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

**MySQL**: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries.

**MongoDB**: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, which counts against it for orders.

**Elasticsearch**: far better text search, with relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store: it has to be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents it is almost always premature.

**ClickHouse**: far better analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Usual setup once you outgrow Postgres alone: Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

Each system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost of specialised systems is not the licence but the time of the people keeping them up.
