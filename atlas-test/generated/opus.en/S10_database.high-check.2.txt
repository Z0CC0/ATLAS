internal knowledge

Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a workload's numbers show you need it.

**Postgres**: covers all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. Real transactions, and orders can't do without them. Built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above. Handles analytics up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL: same as Postgres on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to pick it over Postgres.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. Multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly, which is a bad fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search, with relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store, so it must be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

When you grow, the usual setup is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

**Cost of each added system**: one more synchronisation problem and one more data alignment problem. The real expense is the time of the people keeping it running, not the licence.
