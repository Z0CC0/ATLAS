internal knowledge

Start with **Postgres alone**. Add other systems only when a specific workload demands it, with numbers to prove it.

Postgres covers all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. Real transactions, not negotiable for orders. Built-in text search good enough below a million documents, weak above. Analytics fine up to tens of millions of rows; past that, scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to prefer it.

MongoDB: variable schemas make catalogue easier, but multi-document transactions arrived late and cost. Bad trade with orders in same platform.

Elasticsearch: far better text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. Not authoritative store; must be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better analytics, columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders; point updates its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres authoritative, Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Reach it in steps, only when search or analytics outgrows Postgres.

Each added system brings synchronisation and data alignment problems. **Real cost not licence but time of people keeping it running.** Count that before adding anything.
