Bitcoin came out of a 2008 paper, "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System," signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That name is a pseudonym and nobody has ever identified the person behind it.

The idea grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts such as b-money and bit gold had tried to create digital money but never solved ordering: how to agree on which transaction came first.

The paper tackled double spending, meaning paying with the same money twice, without a central body keeping the ledger. Its solution was a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Changing an old block would mean redoing the proof of work for it and for every block after it. That makes rewriting the past expensive.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That served as a timestamp, proving the block could not predate that day. It was also a statement of intent.

Each block paid its creator a reward of 50 bitcoin at the start. The reward halves every 210,000 blocks, which takes roughly four years. Because of this, the total ever issued approaches 21 million and never goes beyond it.

The first documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011, and the bitcoin Satoshi held have never been moved.
