Bitcoin came from a 2008 paper signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym never identified, titled *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*.

Idea grew out of cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts such as b-money and bit gold had not solved ordering, meaning agreeing which transaction came first. Paper tackled that as double spending: stopping same money being spent twice without central body keeping ledger.

Solution: chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Proof of work makes rewriting past expensive, since attacker would have to redo it for every later block.

First block generated on 3 January 2009. It contains headline of Times article about bank bailout. That reference works both as timestamp, proving block not made earlier, and as statement of intent.

Issuance fixed from start. Block reward begins at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly four years, so total issued tends to 21 million and no further.

First documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid with 10,000 bitcoin.

Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011, and their bitcoin have never been moved.
