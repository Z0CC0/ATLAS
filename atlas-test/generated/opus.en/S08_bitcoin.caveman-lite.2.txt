Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene. People there had tried for years to build digital money. Earlier designs such as b-money and bit gold got part of the way, but none of them solved ordering: how to agree on which transaction came first.

In 2008 a paper titled *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System* appeared under the name Satoshi Nakamoto. The name is a pseudonym, and the person behind it has never been identified. The paper deals with double spending, which means spending the same digital coin twice, and it does this without a central body keeping the ledger.

Its solution is a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Each block requires computational work, and each new block builds on the ones before it. To rewrite a past transaction, an attacker would have to redo the proof of work for that block and for every block after it. That cost is what protects the history.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. The headline proves the block was not created before that date, so it works as a timestamp. It also states what the project was for: money that does not depend on banks being rescued.

The issuance rules were fixed from the start. Each block originally paid a reward of 50 bitcoin. That reward halves every 210,000 blocks, which is roughly every four years. Because of the halvings, the total supply approaches 21 million bitcoin and will never go beyond it.

The first documented commercial transaction took place in 2010, when someone paid 10,000 bitcoin for two pizzas. In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part in public. The bitcoin attributed to Satoshi have never been moved.
