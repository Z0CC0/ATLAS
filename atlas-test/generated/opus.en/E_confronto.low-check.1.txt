**Postgres.** On a small project it covers everything a queue needs, and you add Redis later only if volume actually requires it.

Throughput: `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute, which is plenty for a small project.

Consistency: the job goes in the same transaction as the data. If the write is rolled back, the job never starts.

Durability: you get persistence for free. If the process dies, the queue is still there.

Redis wins on volume and latency, which is under a millisecond. But it's one more service to run, monitor and upgrade. Its persistence also needs setting up: without AOF enabled, a restart loses the queued jobs.

Switch to Redis when your job volume really outgrows Postgres.
