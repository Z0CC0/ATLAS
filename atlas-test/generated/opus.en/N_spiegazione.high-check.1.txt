internal knowledge

Eventual consistency: after write, replicas don't agree immediately, but converge within finite time if writes stop.

Why it exists: trade against availability. System demanding strong consistency must refuse requests when replicas cannot talk.

Where you feel it: reading right after writing. You post comment, reload, don't see it.

How long: same region usually under a second; across regions, seconds.

Common fix: read from primary replica for session that just wrote, not for everyone.

Why you should care: not a defect to eliminate but a choice. Most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

When it's unacceptable: few cases, easy to recognise. Account balances, seat availability, uniqueness of an identifier.
