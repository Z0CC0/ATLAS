Before you pick an architecture, answer these:

**1. Which parts actually change?** Map where changes land in the monolith. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

**2. Where are your transaction boundaries?** If a transaction spans two services, it becomes a distributed problem. Draw any service lines around transactions, not through them.

**3. Don't do a full parallel rewrite.** It almost always fails: the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up. Strangle it instead:
- Put a router in front of the monolith.
- Move one piece at a time.
- Keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

**4. Count the operational cost of microservices.** Every service needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

**5. Consider a modular monolith.** It's the option nobody mentions. You get sharp internal boundaries and a single release, and you can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.

**6. The deciding question isn't technical.** What must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Choose the architecture that makes that possible.
