internal knowledge

**Start with Postgres alone.** Add another system only when a specific workload needs it and you have the numbers to show it.

Postgres: handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. Gives real transactions, which orders can't do without. Built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. Handles analytics up to tens of millions of rows; past that, scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders. Weaker on data types and complex queries, so it gains you nothing over Postgres here.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost performance, which is a poor fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better text search, with relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store, so it must be fed from another database and can't replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

**Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem.** The real cost isn't the licence but the time of the people keeping it running.
