Start with **Postgres alone**. Add another system only when one workload needs it and you have numbers to prove it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, but none of them outstandingly. It gives real transactions, which orders can't do without. Its built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. It carries analytics up to tens of millions of rows; beyond that, scans get slow.

MySQL is equivalent on orders but weaker on data types and complex queries, so it gains you nothing over Postgres.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier because schemas can vary. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, which hurts on orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It is not an authoritative store, so it must be fed from another database and can't replace it. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. It's not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

The usual end state is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

**Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem.** Their real cost is not the licence but the time of the people keeping them up.

When to add each one:

Elasticsearch: when search passes a million documents, or relevance, synonyms or typo tolerance become requirements.

ClickHouse: when analytics passes tens of millions of rows and the scans become measurably slow.
