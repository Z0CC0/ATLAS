Start with one question, and it isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Let that answer pick the architecture.

Before choosing, find out which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

Then map where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction that ends up spanning two services becomes a distributed problem, so draw service lines where transactions don't cross.

Don't do a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until the new piece has been verified.

Count the operational cost before you pick microservices. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider a **modular monolith**, the option nobody mentions. It gives you sharp internal boundaries and a single release, and you can still extract a module the day you genuinely need to.
