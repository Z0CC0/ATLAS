Start with a question that isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? That answer should drive the architecture.

Then map two things in the monolith.

**Which parts actually change.** Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them where they are.

**Where transaction boundaries sit.** A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem, so cut along those boundaries, never across them.

Don't run a full rewrite in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up. Strangle progressively instead: put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until each new piece is verified.

Count the operational cost of microservices honestly. Every service wants releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider the modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It gives you sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
