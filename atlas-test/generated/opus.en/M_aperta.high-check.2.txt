internal knowledge

Start with **what team must be able to do a year from now that it cannot today**. That question is organizational, not technical, and it decides the rest.

Next: which parts of monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Then: where transaction boundaries sit. Transaction spanning two services becomes distributed problem, so draw service lines around transactions, not across them.

Avoid full rewrite running parallel to old system. Almost always fails: old one keeps changing, copy never catches up.

Instead, strangle progressively. Router in front, move one piece at a time, old system stays authoritative until new piece verified.

Microservices cost more in operations: every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and someone looking after it. **Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds benefit.**

Consider modular monolith, option nobody mentions: sharp internal boundaries, one release, and option to extract module the day it's genuinely needed.
