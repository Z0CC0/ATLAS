internal knowledge

Start with one question, and it isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Let that answer pick the architecture.

Before you decide, check four things.

1. **Which parts of the monolith actually change.** Parts that stay stable gain nothing from being split out.

2. **Where your transaction boundaries are.** If a transaction spans two services, it becomes a distributed problem.

3. **How many developers you have.** Every microservice needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and someone who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that operational cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

4. **Whether a modular monolith would do.** Few people mention it, but it gives you sharp internal boundaries and a single release. You can still pull a module out the day you genuinely need to.

On how to migrate: don't run a full rewrite in parallel with the old system. That almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up. Strangle it progressively instead:

1. Put a router in front of the monolith.
2. Move one piece at a time.
3. Keep the old system authoritative for each piece until the new version is verified.
