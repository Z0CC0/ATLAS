**Start with Postgres alone.** Add another system only when a specific workload needs it and you have numbers to show it.

**Postgres:** handles all four workloads acceptably, none of them outstandingly.
- Orders: real transactions. Not negotiable for orders.
- Text search: built in. Good enough below 1 million documents, weak above.
- Analytics: fine up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, scans get slow.

**MySQL:** same as Postgres on orders. Weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to pick it over Postgres.

**MongoDB:** variable schemas make the catalogue easier. But multi-document transactions arrived late and cost performance, which hurts on orders.

**Elasticsearch:** far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store. It must be fed from another database, never replace it. Below 1 million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

**ClickHouse:** far better at analytics. Columnar scans run orders of magnitude faster. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

**Where it usually ends up:** Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

**Cost of each added system:**
- A synchronisation problem.
- A data alignment problem.
- The real cost is not the licence. It's the time of the people keeping it running.

**Triggers to add something:**
- Search over 1 million documents, or relevance, synonyms or typo handling become an issue: add Elasticsearch.
- Analytics past tens of millions of rows with slow scans: add ClickHouse.
- Orders stay on Postgres in every case.
