Local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere, so the application never waits on a log write.

The rule that always holds: writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.

**Files alone:** zero cost, no dependency. But searching means grep, and you have to configure rotation by hand or the disk fills. You also can't search across several machines; you have to connect to each one.

**Managed service:** instant search, alerts and automatic retention. But you pay by volume, and at a thousand requests a minute the volume grows fast. The logs also leave your infrastructure, so you have to strip personal data before they go.

**Database:** searching is familiar and convenient. But putting logs in the application's own database makes two very different workloads compete. Under load, the logs fill caches and connections that the real requests needed.
