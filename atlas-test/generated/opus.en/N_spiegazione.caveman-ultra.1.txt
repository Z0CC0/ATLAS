**Eventual consistency:** after a write, replicas don't agree right away. If writes stop, they converge within a finite time.

**How long:** under 1 second across replicas in the same region. Seconds across regions.

**Why it exists:** it trades consistency for availability. A strongly consistent system must refuse requests when replicas can't talk to each other. An eventually consistent system keeps serving.

**Why you should care:** it hits users who read right after they write. Example: a user posts a comment, reloads the page, and the comment is missing.

**Common fix:** send reads to the primary replica only for the session that just wrote. Other users keep reading from any replica.

**How to think about it:** it's a choice, not a defect to remove. Most systems that scale accept it on purpose.

**When you can't accept it:** these cases are few and easy to spot:
- account balances
- seat availability
- unique identifiers

Outside those cases, accept it and fix read-after-write per session.
