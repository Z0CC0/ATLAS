**Postgres.** Use Redis only once your volume actually needs it.

Postgres with `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute. That's more than enough for a small project.

The job sits in the same transaction as your data. If the write rolls back, the job rolls back with it, so a job never starts for data that doesn't exist.

Persistence comes free. If the process dies, the queue is still there.

Redis handles far more volume and has latency under a millisecond. It also costs you more:

One more service to run, monitor and upgrade.

Persistence needs configuring. Without AOF enabled, a restart loses the queued jobs.

Start with Postgres. Move to Redis when job volume outgrows what Postgres can handle.
