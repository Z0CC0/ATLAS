**Postgres**, for a small project. Switch to Redis only when volume genuinely demands it.

With `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, Postgres handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute. For a small project that's plenty.

Job sits in same transaction as data, so a job can never start while its matching write gets rolled back.

Persistence comes free: if the process dies, the queue survives.

Redis handles far more volume and has lower latency, under a millisecond. It also means one more service to run, monitor and upgrade. Persistence needs configuring there: without AOF enabled, a restart loses queued jobs.
