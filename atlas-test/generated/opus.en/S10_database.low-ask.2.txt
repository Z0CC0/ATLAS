**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when one specific workload outgrows it and you have numbers to show that.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, though none of them outstandingly. It gives real transactions, and on orders those are not negotiable. Its built-in text search holds up below a million documents and gets weak above that. Analytics run fine up to tens of millions of rows, after which scans get slow.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but is weaker on data types and complex queries. It has no advantage here.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier because it handles variable schemas. But multi-document transactions arrived late and come at a cost, which is a bad trade when orders sit in the same database.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It is not an authoritative store, though. It has to be fed from another database, not replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead of Postgres. It is not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

If you outgrow Postgres, the usual setup keeps Postgres as the authoritative store and feeds Elasticsearch and ClickHouse from it. Each system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost of these specialised systems is not the licence but the time of the people keeping them up.

In practice: use Postgres for orders from day one. Add Elasticsearch when the catalogue passes about a million documents and search quality suffers. Add ClickHouse when analytics reach tens of millions of rows and the scans get slow.
