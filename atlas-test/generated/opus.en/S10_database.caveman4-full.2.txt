**Pick Postgres. Start with it alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse later, only when one workload needs it and you have numbers to show it.

**Why Postgres is the base**
- Orders need real transactions, and that is not negotiable. Postgres gives you them.
- It handles all four of your workloads acceptably, though none of them outstandingly.
- Text search is built in. It is good enough below a million documents and weak above that.
- Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows. Beyond that, scans get slow.

**The other options**
- **MySQL**: as good as Postgres for orders. It is weaker on data types and complex queries, so it doesn't win anywhere for you.
- **MongoDB**: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. But multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly. That is a bad trade when orders are the part you can't compromise on.
- **Elasticsearch**: far better at text search, with relevance ranking, synonyms and typo tolerance. It is not an authoritative store. You feed it from another database; it does not replace one.
- **ClickHouse**: far better at analytics. Its columnar scans are orders of magnitude faster. It is not suited to orders because point updates are its weak spot.

**Where you may end up**
The usual setup is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Each system you add brings two problems: keeping it in sync and keeping its data aligned with Postgres.

**When to add each one**
- **Elasticsearch**: when the catalogue passes about a million documents, or search quality is measurably hurting you. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.
- **ClickHouse**: when analytics pass tens of millions of rows and scan times are a real problem.

**What it really costs**
The licence is not the main cost of a specialised system. The main cost is the time your people spend keeping it running. Count that before adding anything.
