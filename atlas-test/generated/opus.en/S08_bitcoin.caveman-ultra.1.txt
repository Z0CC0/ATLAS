Bitcoin grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts like b-money and bit gold came first. They never solved ordering, meaning agreement on which transaction came first.

**Paper, 2008.** A paper titled *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System* appeared, signed Satoshi Nakamoto. Nobody has ever identified who used that pseudonym. The paper tackled double spending: stopping someone from spending the same money twice when no central body keeps the ledger.

**Solution.** Transactions go into a chain of blocks, and a proof of work sets their order. The proof of work makes rewriting the past expensive. Changing an old block means redoing the work for every block after it.

**Genesis block, 3 January 2009.** The first block contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That headline proves the block was not made before that date, so it works as a timestamp. It also states the project's purpose: money that doesn't depend on banks.

**Issuance.** The block reward started at 50 bitcoin. It halves every 210,000 blocks, which is about every four years. Because of this, the total supply approaches 21 million and never goes past it.

**First real use, 2010.** The first documented commercial transaction was two pizzas, paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

**Satoshi leaves, 2011.** Satoshi stopped taking part in public that year. Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
