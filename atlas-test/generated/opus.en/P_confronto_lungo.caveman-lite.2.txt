Use local files plus a collector that ships them elsewhere. At about a thousand requests a minute, this setup means your application never waits on a log write.

Whatever you pick, one rule always holds: writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.

Each option on its own has a problem.

**Files only.** Files cost nothing and add no dependency. Searching them means grep, though. You have to configure rotation by hand, or the disk fills. You also can't search across several machines. You have to connect to each one separately.

**Managed service.** You get instant search, alerts and automatic retention. The cost is the catch, because you pay by volume. At a thousand requests a minute, that volume grows fast. Your logs also leave your own infrastructure, so you must strip personal data before they go.

**Database.** Searching logs with queries feels familiar and convenient. But putting logs into your application's own database makes two very different workloads compete. Under load, log writes fill caches and use connections that your real requests needed.

Writing to local files keeps the request path fast and cheap. The collector then moves the logs to wherever you search them. That can be a managed service if you want its search and alerts. If you send them there, strip personal data in the collector first. Also watch the volume so the bill stays under control. Set up rotation on the local files so the disk doesn't fill.
