internal knowledge

Bitcoin grew out of the cypherpunk scene. That scene had already tried digital money with b-money and bit gold, but neither solved ordering, meaning how to put transactions in a sequence everyone agrees on.

In 2008 a paper appeared titled *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*. It was signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym that has never been identified. The paper set out to stop double spending without a central body keeping the ledger.

Its answer was a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Changing an old block means redoing the proof of work for that block and for every block after it. That makes rewriting the past expensive.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. The headline works both as a timestamp and as a statement of intent.

New bitcoin come from the block reward. It started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. Because of this, the total ever issued tends to 21 million and never goes beyond it.

The first documented commercial transaction was in 2010, when someone paid 10,000 bitcoin for two pizzas.

Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011, and Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
