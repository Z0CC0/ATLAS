internal knowledge

Bitcoin grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts at digital cash, such as b-money and bit gold, never solved one problem: how to agree on the order of transactions.

In 2008 a paper appeared titled *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*, signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That name was a pseudonym, and nobody has ever identified who used it. The paper tackled double spending: stopping the same money from being spent twice when no central body keeps the ledger.

Its solution: a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Each block carries computational work, so rewriting the past means redoing that work for the altered block and for every block after it. That cost protects the history.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contained the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That reference worked both as a timestamp, proving the block could not predate it, and as a statement of intent.

Issuance followed a fixed rule. Block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. Total supply therefore tends toward 21 million and never goes beyond it.

The first documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

In 2011 Satoshi stopped taking part publicly. The bitcoin attributed to Satoshi have never been moved.
