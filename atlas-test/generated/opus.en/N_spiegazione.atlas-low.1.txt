Eventual consistency means that after a write, replicas don't agree right away. If writes stop, they converge within a finite time.

**Why you should care:** it hits whoever reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

How long the gap lasts: under a second across replicas in the same region, and seconds across regions.

Why systems accept it: the trade-off is against availability. A system that demands strong consistency has to refuse requests when its replicas cannot talk to each other. So eventual consistency isn't a defect to eliminate but a choice, and most systems that scale make it deliberately.

Common fix for the reload problem: read from the primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

**Where it's unacceptable:** the cases are few and easy to recognise: account balances, seat availability, and uniqueness of an identifier.
