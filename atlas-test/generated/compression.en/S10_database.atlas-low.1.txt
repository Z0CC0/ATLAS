Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse later, only when a specific workload proves it needs one, numbers in hand.

Postgres handles orders, catalogue, search and analytics all acceptably, none outstandingly. On orders it gives real transactions, non-negotiable for that workload. Text search built in, fine below a million documents, weak above. Analytics fine up to tens of millions of rows, then scans slow down.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to prefer it here.

MongoDB eases catalogue work through variable schemas, but multi-document transactions arrived late and cost — bad fit for orders.

Elasticsearch beats everything on text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store though — needs feeding from another database, never replaces one. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

ClickHouse beats everything on analytics, columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Weak at point updates, so not for orders.

Usual setup once you outgrow Postgres alone: Postgres as authoritative store, Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it for search and analytics respectively. Every system added brings a sync problem and a data-alignment problem. Real cost of specialised systems isn't licence, it's the people-time keeping them in sync.
