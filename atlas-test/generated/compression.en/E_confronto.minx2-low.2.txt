Postgres, until volume really calls for Redis.

With `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, Postgres handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute.

The job sits in the same transaction as your data. A job can never start while its matching write gets rolled back.

You get persistence for free. If the process dies, the queue is still there.

Redis handles far more volume and has lower latency, under a millisecond. But it's one more service to run, monitor and upgrade. You also have to set up persistence yourself: without AOF enabled, a restart loses the queued jobs.

Move to Redis only when your volume genuinely demands it.
