Bitcoin came from a 2008 paper signed Satoshi Nakamoto, pseudonym never identified, titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System".

Roots lie in cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts such as b-money and bit gold had tried digital cash but never solved ordering: agreeing which transaction came first.

Problem paper tackled: double spending, meaning same coin spent twice, without central body keeping ledger. Solution: chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting past becomes expensive, because attacker would have to redo proof of work for every later block too.

First block generated on 3 January 2009. It contains headline of Times article about bank bailout, which works both as timestamp and as statement of intent.

Issuance fixed from start. Block reward begins at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly four years, so total issued tends to 21 million and no further.

First documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid with 10,000 bitcoin.

Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011. Their bitcoin have never been moved.
