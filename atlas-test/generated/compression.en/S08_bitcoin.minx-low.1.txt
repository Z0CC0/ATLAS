Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene. It solved a problem that earlier attempts like b-money and bit gold had left open: agreeing on the order of transactions without anyone in charge.

In 2008 a paper titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" appeared, signed Satoshi Nakamoto. That name is a pseudonym, and the person behind it has never been identified. The paper tackled double spending: how to stop the same money being spent twice when no central body keeps the ledger.

Its answer was a chain of blocks, ordered by proof of work. Changing an old block would mean redoing the work for that block and for every block after it. That makes rewriting the past expensive.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. The headline proves the block wasn't made before that date, and it also states what the project was for.

Issuance is fixed in the rules. The reward for each block started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, which is roughly every four years. Because of this, the total supply approaches 21 million and never goes past it.

The first documented purchase came in 2010, when someone paid 10,000 bitcoin for two pizzas.

Satoshi stopped taking part in public in 2011. The bitcoin Satoshi held have never been moved.
