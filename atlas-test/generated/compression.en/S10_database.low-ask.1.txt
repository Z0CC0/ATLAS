Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. Gives real transactions — on orders, non-negotiable. Built-in text search good enough below million documents, weak above. Carries analytics fine up to tens of millions rows, past that scans slow.

MySQL equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. No advantage over Postgres for your case.

MongoDB makes catalogue easier via variable schemas, but multi-document transactions arrived late and cost — a liability for orders specifically.

Elasticsearch far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store though — needs feeding from another database, not replacing one.

ClickHouse far better at analytics, columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders — point updates its weak spot.

Usual setup: Postgres as authoritative store, Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system added brings a sync problem and a data-alignment problem — that's the real cost, not licence, but time of people keeping them up.

Practical rule for you: start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload demands it, numbers in hand — adding Elasticsearch below million documents almost always premature.
