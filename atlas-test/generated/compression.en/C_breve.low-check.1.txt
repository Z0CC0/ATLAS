No — B-tree index normally goes unused on a column with just three distinct values, since the planner favors a sequential scan whenever the filter matches roughly more than 5-10% of rows, and with only three values each one is far above that threshold. Meanwhile the index still costs you writes and disk space with no payoff. The one case where indexing pays off is a partial index built on the rare value, if one of the three is much less common than the others.
