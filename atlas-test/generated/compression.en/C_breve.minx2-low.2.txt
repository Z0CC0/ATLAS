No, usually not. The planner normally won't use a B-tree index on a low-cardinality column. It picks a sequential scan when a filter matches more than roughly 5-10 per cent of the rows, and with only three values most filters match far more than that. You'd still pay for the index in slower writes and disk space.

The exception is when one of the values is rare. A partial index covering just that value does work.
