No, usually not. On a column with low cardinality, the planner normally won't use a B-tree index. Each value matches about a third of the rows, and once a filter selects more than roughly 5–10 per cent of a table, a sequential scan is cheaper. The index would still add cost to every write and take up disk space.

There's one exception. If one of the three values is rare and you query for it, a partial index on that value alone does get used.
