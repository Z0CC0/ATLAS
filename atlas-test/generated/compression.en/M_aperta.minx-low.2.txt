Before choosing any architecture, find out **which part of the monolith actually changes**. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Next, map where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction that spans two services becomes a distributed problem, so draw service lines where transactions already end.

Don't rewrite everything in parallel with the old system. That almost always fails, because the old one keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. Keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Look seriously at the modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It gives you sharp internal boundaries and a single release, and you can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.

The deciding question isn't technical: **what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today?** Pick the architecture that answers that.
