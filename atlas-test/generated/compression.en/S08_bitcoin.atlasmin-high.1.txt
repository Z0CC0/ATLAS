2008: paper signed Satoshi Nakamoto, pseudonym, never identified. Title: "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System". Problem tackled: double spending without central ledger-keeper. Solution: chain of blocks ordered by proof of work — rewriting past blocks expensive, requires redoing work for every later block.

3 January 2009: first block generated, contains headline of Times article on bank bailout. Serves as timestamp and statement of intent.

Reward: starts 50 BTC, halves every 210,000 blocks (~4 years). Total issuance tends to 21 million, no further.

2010: first documented commercial transaction, two pizzas for 10,000 BTC.

2011: Satoshi stops public participation. Those early coins never moved since.

Project grows from cypherpunk scene, after earlier attempts (b-money, bit gold) that failed to solve ordering.
