First: which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from splitting.

Second: where transaction boundaries sit — a transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Skip full rewrite in parallel with the old system — old one keeps changing, copy never catches up. Instead: progressive strangling. Router in front, move one piece at a time, old system stays authoritative until new one's verified.

Microservices cost more in ops — every service wants releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, an owner. Below five devs, that cost usually beats the benefit.

Modular monolith: sharp internal boundaries, one release, option to extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.

Real question isn't technical: what must the team do in a year that it can't today.
