Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree immediately, but they converge within finite time once writes stop.

It matters to whoever reads right after writing. User posts comment, reloads, and doesn't see it.

Usual fix: read from primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

The gap is short. Across replicas in the same region, convergence typically takes under a second. Across regions it rises to seconds.

You pay this to keep availability. A system demanding strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas cannot talk to each other.

So it's not a defect to eliminate but a choice. Most systems that scale accept it on purpose.

It's unacceptable in only a few cases, and they're easy to recognise: account balances, seat availability, uniqueness of an identifier.
