Postgres.

SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED handles up to ~1000 jobs/min, plenty for small scale. Job lives in same transaction as the data — never starts if the write rolls back. Process dies, queue survives, no extra config.

Redis wins on raw throughput and latency (sub-ms), but costs another service to run, monitor, upgrade — and persistence isn't automatic: no AOF, restart wipes the queue.

Switch to Redis only once volume actually demands it.
