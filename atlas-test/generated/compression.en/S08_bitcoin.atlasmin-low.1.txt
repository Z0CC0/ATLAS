In 2008, paper appears signed Satoshi Nakamoto — pseudonym, never identified. Titled Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System.

Problem it tackles: double spending without central body keeping ledger. Solution: chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Proof of work makes rewriting past expensive — would have to be redone for every later block.

First block generated 3 January 2009, contains headline of Times article about bank bailout. Works both as timestamp and statement of intent.

Block reward starts at 50 bitcoin, halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly four years. Total issued tends to 21 million, no further.

First documented commercial transaction from 2010: two pizzas paid with 10,000 bitcoin. Satoshi stops taking part publicly in 2011 — those bitcoin never moved since.

Project grows out of cypherpunk scene, after earlier attempts like b-money and bit gold that didn't solve ordering.
