Eventual consistency is what you get when a system copies your data across multiple replicas and doesn't force them to agree the instant something changes. You write, and for a little while the replicas disagree with each other — but if no new writes come in, they converge on the same value within a finite time. Nothing is lost or wrong forever; it's just not synchronized immediately.

Why should you care? Because the alternative — strong consistency — has a real cost: a system that insists all replicas agree before answering has to refuse requests when those replicas can't talk to each other. So this is a trade against availability, not a free correctness upgrade. Every system that replicates data picks a side, whether the team realizes it or not.

The place this actually bites you is the read-after-write case: someone posts a comment, reloads the page, and doesn't see their own comment. That's not a bug in the traditional sense — it's the replica they landed on not having caught up yet. In practice this window is short: replicas in the same region typically converge in under a second, across regions it can stretch to a few seconds. Annoying, but brief.

The standard fix isn't to abandon eventual consistency everywhere — it's to special-case the person who just wrote. Route that specific session's reads to the primary replica for a while, so the author sees their own comment immediately, while everyone else keeps reading from whichever replica is nearest or cheapest. You get the availability benefit without the "did my post disappear?" confusion.

So don't treat eventual consistency as a flaw to engineer away — most systems that need to scale choose it on purpose, because demanding strong consistency everywhere would mean rejecting requests any time replicas lose contact. The cases where you genuinely can't accept it are narrow and easy to spot: account balances, seat availability, anything where uniqueness of an identifier matters. Those need strong consistency. Almost everything else — comments, likes, view counts, profile updates — can tolerate the lag, and accepting that lag is what lets the system stay available.
