**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload demands it, with numbers in hand.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. Real transactions, which on orders are not negotiable. Built-in text search good enough below a million documents, weak above. Analytics fine up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries. No reason to prefer it.

MongoDB makes catalogue easier with variable schemas. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, a problem for orders.

Elasticsearch far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store: must be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse far better at analytics, columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders: point updates are its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres as authoritative store, Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Each added system brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. Real cost is not the licence but the time of the people keeping them up.
