No. Anything that reaches the browser is readable — obfuscation falls apart the moment someone opens dev tools or looks at the network requests.

The key belongs on your server. Your frontend calls an endpoint on your own backend, and that backend calls the service with the key.

Put a rate limit on that backend endpoint, or it becomes a free way for anyone to spend your key's quota.

If this key has already been in frontend code that shipped, treat it as compromised and rotate it now — don't just remove it. And if it was ever committed, removing it from the code isn't enough: it's still sitting in the repo's history, and anyone with a clone already has it.
