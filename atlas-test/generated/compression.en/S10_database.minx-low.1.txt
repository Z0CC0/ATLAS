Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a workload's numbers show you need it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, though it excels at none of them. It gives real transactions, and on orders you can't do without them. Its built-in text search works below a million documents and gets weak above that. It carries analytics up to tens of millions of rows, and past that point scans get slow.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but is weaker on data types and complex queries. It has no reason to win here.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier because it allows variable schemas. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost you, which is a poor fit for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It isn't an authoritative store, so it has to be fed from another database, never replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. It's not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

The usual end state is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost isn't the licence. It's the time of the people keeping these systems up.

Add Elasticsearch when search passes a million documents or relevance becomes a real problem. Add ClickHouse when analytics goes past tens of millions of rows and scans slow down. Until then, Postgres alone covers orders, catalogue, search and analytics.
