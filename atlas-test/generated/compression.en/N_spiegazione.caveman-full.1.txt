Eventual consistency: after write, replicas disagree briefly, converge within finite time if writes stop.

Trade against availability. Strong consistency demands replicas agree now — so system refuse requests when replicas can't talk. Eventual consistency skip that, stay available, accept temporary disagreement instead.

Bite you right after your own write: post comment, reload, comment gone. Convergence usually fast — under a second same region, few seconds cross-region — but that gap enough for user think their write got lost.

Fix: read-your-writes — route the session that just wrote back to primary replica. Not everyone, only writer's own session.

Not bug, choice. Most systems that scale pick this deliberately over strong consistency's availability cost.

Few cases where unacceptable: account balances, seat availability, uniqueness checks (username, order ID). There, stale read means real damage — double-book seat, double-spend balance. Those need strong consistency; everything else, eventual usually fine.
