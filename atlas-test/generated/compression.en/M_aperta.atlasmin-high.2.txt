Check which part of the monolith actually changes. Stable parts gain nothing from splitting.

Check where the transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Skip a full rewrite in parallel with the old system — it almost always fails, since the old one keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Use progressive strangling instead: router in front, move one piece at a time, old system stays authoritative until the new one is verified.

Microservices cost more in ops — every service wants its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, and someone to own it.

Below five developers, that operational cost usually exceeds the benefit.

Consider the modular monolith, the option nobody brings up: sharp internal boundaries, one release, option to extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.

The deciding question isn't technical: what must your team be able to do in a year that it can't do today.
