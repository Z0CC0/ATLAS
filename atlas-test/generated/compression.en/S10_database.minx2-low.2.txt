**Start with Postgres alone.** Add a specialised system only when a specific workload needs it and you have the numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably and none of them outstandingly. It gives real transactions, and on orders those aren't negotiable. Its built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, and after that the scans get slow.

MySQL matches Postgres on orders but is weaker on data types and complex queries, so it gains you nothing here.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier because it handles variable schemas. But multi-document transactions arrived late and cost a lot, which works against it for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It isn't an authoritative store, though. It has to be fed from another database, not replace one. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster. It isn't for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

If you outgrow one system, the usual setup is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost isn't the licence. It's the time of the people keeping those systems running.

In practice: go with Postgres now. Watch the document count on search and the row count on analytics. When one of them passes its threshold and it actually hurts, add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse for that workload only.
