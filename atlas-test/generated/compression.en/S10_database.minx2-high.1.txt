Start with **Postgres alone**. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when one workload demands it, with numbers in hand.

Postgres: acceptable on all four workloads, outstanding on none. Real transactions, which orders can't do without. Built-in text search good enough below a million documents, weak above. Analytics fine up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost, so it's a poor fit for orders.

Elasticsearch: far better text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store. It must be fed from another database, not replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better analytics, columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres as the authoritative store, Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Each system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost isn't the licence. It's **the time of the people keeping it up**.
