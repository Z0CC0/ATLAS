Decide first what team must be able to do a year from now that it can't today. That question settles architecture more than any technical argument.

First thing to learn about monolith is which parts actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split out.

Second is where transaction boundaries sit. Transaction spanning two services becomes distributed problem, so draw service lines where transactions already end.

Count operational cost honestly. Every microservice needs its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person looking after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds benefit.

Consider **modular monolith**, option nobody mentions: sharp internal boundaries, one release, and freedom to extract module the day it's genuinely needed.

Don't rewrite everything in parallel with old system. That almost always fails, because old one keeps changing and copy never catches up. Strangle it progressively instead: put router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep **old system authoritative until new piece is verified**.
