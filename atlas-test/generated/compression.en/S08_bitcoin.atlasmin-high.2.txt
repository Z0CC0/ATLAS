2008: paper signed Satoshi Nakamoto, pseudonym never identified — "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System." Problem: double spending without central ledger-keeper. Solution: chain of blocks ordered by proof of work — rewriting past means redoing every later block, expensive.

First block, 3 January 2009, contains Times headline on bank bailout — timestamp and statement of intent.

Reward starts 50 bitcoin, halves every 210,000 blocks (roughly four years); total tends to 21 million, no further.

2010: first documented commercial transaction, two pizzas for 10,000 bitcoin.

2011: Satoshi stops public participation; those coins never moved.

Project grew from cypherpunk scene, after b-money, bit gold — earlier attempts that didn't solve ordering.
