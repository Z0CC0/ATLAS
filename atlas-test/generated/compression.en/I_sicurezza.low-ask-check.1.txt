No. Anything sent to the browser can be read — obfuscation breaks the moment someone opens dev tools or watches the network requests.

Keep the key on the server. Have the frontend call your own backend endpoint, and let that backend call the third-party service with the key.

Put a rate limit on that endpoint — without one, it's a free tunnel to spend your key's quota.

If this key has already shipped to the frontend at any point, treat it as compromised now and rotate it. Deleting it from the code isn't enough — it stays in your git history, and anyone who already cloned the repo already has it.
