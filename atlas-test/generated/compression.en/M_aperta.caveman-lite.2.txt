Before rewriting, worth working through in order:

**What actually changes.** Find which parts of the monolith see real change velocity. Stable parts gain nothing from being split out — splitting them is pure overhead with no payoff.

**Where transactions sit.** Map your transaction boundaries first. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed transaction problem — much harder than in-process consistency.

**Don't do a parallel rewrite.** Building a new system alongside the old one almost always fails: the old one keeps changing, and the rewrite never catches up.

**Strangle it instead.** Put a router in front of the monolith, migrate one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until each new piece is verified. Progressive, not parallel.

**Mind the operational cost of microservices.** Every service wants its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, and an owner. That's real ongoing cost, not a one-time setup fee.

**Team size matters.** Below five developers, the operational overhead of microservices almost always exceeds the benefit.

**Consider the modular monolith.** Sharp internal module boundaries, one release, one deploy — but any module can be extracted later the day it's genuinely needed. Often the option nobody proposes.

**The real deciding question isn't technical.** Ask: what does the team need to be able to do in a year that it can't do today? Let that answer drive the architecture, not the reverse.
