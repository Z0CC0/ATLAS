Bitcoin began in 2008 with a paper signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym never identified: "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System".

It grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts, b-money and bit gold, never solved how to put transactions in order.

The problem it tackled was double spending with no central body keeping the ledger.

Its answer was a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting the past costs too much, because the work would have to be redone for every later block.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009 and carries the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That headline is both a timestamp and a statement of intent.

The block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. Total supply approaches 21 million and never goes past it.

The first documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011, and Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
