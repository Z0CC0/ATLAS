Before architecture: figure out which part of the monolith actually changes. Stable parts gain nothing from being split out.

Then look at where transaction boundaries sit — a transaction spanning two services turns into a distributed problem, so that's often the real constraint, not the tech stack.

Skip the "rewrite in parallel, cut over later" plan — old system keeps changing while you rebuild, copy never catches up, almost always fails. Progressive strangling works better: router in front, move one piece at a time, old system stays authoritative until each new piece is verified.

Weigh microservices against team size honestly: every service wants its own releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, someone responsible for it. Below five developers that operational cost almost always beats whatever benefit you get.

Modular monolith is worth considering seriously — sharp internal boundaries, one release, and you keep the option to extract a module the day it's genuinely needed. Nobody brings it up, but it often fits better than either extreme.

Underneath all of it, the real question isn't technical: what does the team need to be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? That answer should drive the architecture choice, not the reverse.
