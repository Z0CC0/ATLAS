First: know which part of the monolith actually changes. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Second: map where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

A full rewrite running in parallel with the old system almost always fails — old keeps changing, copy never catches up.

Alternative: strangle progressively. Router in front, move one piece at a time, old stays authoritative until new is verified.

Microservices cost more in ops — every service wants releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, someone to own it. Below five developers, that cost usually exceeds the benefit.

Modular monolith is the option nobody mentions: sharp internal boundaries, one release, option to extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.

Real question isn't technical: what must your team do in a year that it can't today.
