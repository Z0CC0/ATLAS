At a thousand requests a minute, write logs to local files, plus a collector shipping them elsewhere. The rule that always holds: writing the log must not sit in the critical path of the request.

Files cost nothing, need no dependency, but you configure rotation by hand or the disk fills, and searching means grep, with no cross-machine search — you connect to each one.

A managed service gives instant search, alerts, automatic retention, but you pay by volume, and at this rate volume grows fast; the logs also leave your infrastructure, so personal data has to be stripped before they go.

The database is familiar and convenient for search, but writing logs into the application's own database puts two very different workloads in competition — under load, logs fill the caches and connections the real requests needed.

So: files locally, a collector ships them out, and the application never waits on a log write.
