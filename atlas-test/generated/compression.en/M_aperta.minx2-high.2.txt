Decide first what the team must be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today. That question isn't technical, and it should settle the architecture.

Which parts of the monolith actually change? Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Where do transaction boundaries sit? A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Avoid a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old one keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle progressively instead. Put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until the new one is verified.

Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It gives you sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
