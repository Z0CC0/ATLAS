No.

Anything reaching the browser is readable — obfuscation breaks the moment someone opens dev tools or checks network requests.

The key belongs on your server. The frontend calls your own backend endpoint, and that endpoint calls the service, keeping the key out of the browser entirely.

Rate-limit that endpoint, otherwise it's a free way to burn through your key's quota on anyone's behalf.

If you've already shipped a key to the frontend, treat it as compromised right now — rotate it. Removing it from the code afterward isn't enough: it's still sitting in your repository history, and anyone who already cloned the repo already has it.
