Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts such as b-money and bit gold never solved the problem of ordering transactions.

In 2008 a paper appeared, signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym that has never been identified. Its title was "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System". The problem it took on was double spending without a central body keeping the ledger.

The solution was a chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting the past is expensive because the work would have to be redone for every later block.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That reference works both as a timestamp and as a statement of intent.

The block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. The total issued approaches 21 million and never goes beyond it.

The first documented commercial transaction came in 2010, when two pizzas were bought for 10,000 bitcoin.

Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011. Their bitcoin have never been moved.
