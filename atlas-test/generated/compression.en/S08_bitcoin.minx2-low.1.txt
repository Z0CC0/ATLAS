Bitcoin came out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts such as b-money and bit gold never solved how to put transactions in order.

In 2008 a paper appeared, titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System". It was signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym that has never been identified. The paper tackled double spending: how to stop the same money being spent twice when no central body keeps the ledger.

Its solution was a chain of blocks, put in order by proof of work. Proof of work makes rewriting the past expensive, because an attacker would have to redo it for every later block as well.

The first block was generated on 3 January 2009. It contains the headline of a Times article about a bank bailout. That reference works both as a timestamp and as a statement of intent.

The block reward started at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly every four years. So the total ever issued approaches 21 million and never goes past it.

The first documented commercial transaction came in 2010: two pizzas paid for with 10,000 bitcoin.

Satoshi stopped taking part publicly in 2011, and Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
