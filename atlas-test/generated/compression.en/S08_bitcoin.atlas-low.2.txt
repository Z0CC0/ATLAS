2008, paper appears signed Satoshi Nakamoto, pseudonym never identified. Title: "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System." Problem tackled: double spending without central body keeping ledger. Solution: chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Proof of work makes rewriting past expensive, since redo needed for every later block.

3 January 2009, first block generated, contains headline of Times article about bank bailout — works both as timestamp and statement of intent. Block reward starts at 50 bitcoin, halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly four years. Total issued tends toward 21 million, no further.

2010: first documented commercial transaction, two pizzas paid with 10,000 bitcoin. 2011: Satoshi stops taking part publicly; those bitcoin never moved since.

Project grows out of cypherpunk scene, after earlier attempts like b-money and bit gold, which didn't solve ordering.
