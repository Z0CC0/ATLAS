Postgres for orders, non-negotiable — real transactions.

For the rest, none of it dominates:

- Catalogue: Postgres fine. Variable schemas easier in MongoDB, but multi-document transactions there arrived late and cost — don't take that tradeoff just for schema flexibility.
- Text search: Postgres built-in is fine below a million documents, weak above. Elasticsearch is far better — relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance — but it's not authoritative, needs feeding from a real database, and adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.
- Analytics: Postgres holds up to tens of millions of rows, then scans slow. ClickHouse is far ahead — orders of magnitude — but weak on point updates, so it's not for orders.

MySQL: equivalent to Postgres on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries — no reason to prefer it here.

Practical rule: start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload demands it and you have numbers proving it, not preemptively. The usual endpoint is Postgres as the authoritative store feeding Elasticsearch and ClickHouse.

Cost to weigh before adding either: every extra system brings a sync problem and a data-alignment problem. The real cost isn't licensing, it's the ongoing engineering time to keep them up.
