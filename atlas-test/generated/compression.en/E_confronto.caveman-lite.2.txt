Postgres better fit here.

At thousand jobs per minute or less, `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles it fine. Bonus: job lives in same transaction as data write, so job never starts if write rolls back. Process dies, queue still there, no extra config needed.

Redis wins on raw volume and latency (sub-millisecond), but costs you: one more service to run/monitor/upgrade, and persistence isn't automatic — without AOF enabled, restart wipes queued jobs.

For small project, go Postgres. Move to Redis only when volume genuinely demands it.
