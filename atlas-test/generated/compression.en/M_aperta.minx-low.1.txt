Start with a question that isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Architecture follows from that answer.

Then look at which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

Find where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem, so draw boundaries where transactions already close.

Don't run a full rewrite in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old one keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Price the operations before choosing microservices. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and someone who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider the **modular monolith**, the option nobody mentions. It gives you sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
