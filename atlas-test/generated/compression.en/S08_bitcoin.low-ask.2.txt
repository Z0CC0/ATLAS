2008: paper appears, signed Satoshi Nakamoto — pseudonym, never identified. Title: Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System. Tackles double spending without central body keeping ledger — solution: chain of blocks ordered by proof of work, making rewriting past expensive since every later block would need redoing too.

3 January 2009: first block generated, containing headline from Times article on bank bailout — works both as timestamp and statement of intent.

Reward starts at 50 bitcoin, halves every 210,000 blocks (roughly four years), total issued tending toward 21 million, no further.

2010: first documented commercial transaction — two pizzas, 10,000 bitcoin. Satoshi stops participating publicly 2011; those bitcoin never moved since.

Project grows out of cypherpunk scene, following earlier attempts — b-money, bit gold — that didn't solve ordering.
