**Main question isn't technical:** what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Architecture should serve that answer.

First, find which parts of monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Second, map transaction boundaries. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Avoid full rewrite running in parallel with old system. Almost always fails: old one keeps changing, copy never catches up.

Strangle progressively instead: router in front, move one piece at a time, old system stays authoritative until new one verified.

Microservices cost more in operations: every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, and a person looking after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds benefit.

**Consider modular monolith**, option nobody mentions: sharp internal boundaries, one release, and freedom to extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
