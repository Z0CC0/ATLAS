The RTX 5080 and RTX 4080 are a generational step apart, and the gap shows up differently depending on what you're doing with the card.

Under the hood, the 5080 runs on Nvidia's newer Blackwell architecture, while the 4080 uses Ada Lovelace. That shift also brings faster memory: the 5080 uses GDDR7 instead of GDDR6X, which pushes memory bandwidth from around 717 GB/s up to about 960 GB/s.

Where it matters most for you depends on what you play. If you care about traditional rasterized performance (no upscaling or frame generation tricks), the 5080 is only modestly ahead — roughly 10-15% faster than the 4080. The much bigger leap is in frame generation, where the 5080 pulls noticeably further ahead — so the upgrade pays off most in titles that support it, less so in games you run natively.

On the practical side, power draw goes up a bit, from 320W to 360W rated, but the connector situation doesn't change — both still use the 16-pin power connector, so you won't need a different cable or adapter if you're upgrading from a 4080.

**Bottom line:** if you mainly play games without frame generation support, the 5080 is a modest upgrade, not a dramatic one. If you play titles that lean on frame generation, the difference is much bigger.
