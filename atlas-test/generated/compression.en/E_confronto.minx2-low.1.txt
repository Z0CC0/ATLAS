Postgres, until volume really demands Redis.

With `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, Postgres handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute.

Jobs live in the same transaction as your data. A job never starts if the write it depends on gets rolled back.

Persistence comes free. If the process dies, the queue survives.

Redis handles far more volume, with latency under a millisecond. But it adds one more service to run, monitor and upgrade. It also needs persistence configured: without AOF enabled, a restart loses queued jobs.

Switch to Redis only when your volume goes past what Postgres can handle.
