Eventual consistency: after write, replicas don't agree right away, but converge within finite time once writes stop.

Why care: it hits whoever reads right after writing. User posts comment, reloads, doesn't see it.

Not a defect to eliminate but a choice. Trade is against availability: system demanding strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas can't talk to each other. Most systems that scale accept eventual consistency deliberately.

Window usually short: under a second across replicas in same region, rising to seconds across regions.

Common fix: route reads to primary replica for session that just wrote, not for everyone. That user sees their own comment, and everyone else keeps reading from nearby replicas.

Cases where it's unacceptable are few and easy to recognise: account balances, seat availability, uniqueness of an identifier. Those need strong consistency. For the rest, eventual consistency is usually the right default.
