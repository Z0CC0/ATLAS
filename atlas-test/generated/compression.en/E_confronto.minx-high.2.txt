Postgres, and move to Redis only when volume genuinely demands it.

Postgres with `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute.

Job and data share one transaction, so a job never starts when its write rolled back.

Persistence for free: if the process dies, the queue is still there.

Redis handles far more volume, with latency under a millisecond.

It's also one more service to run, monitor and upgrade.

Its persistence needs configuring: without AOF enabled, a restart loses queued jobs.
