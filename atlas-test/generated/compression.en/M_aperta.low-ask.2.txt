Start by mapping which parts of monolith actually change over time — stable parts gain nothing from split, don't touch them.

Next, trace where transaction boundaries sit. Transaction spanning two services turns into distributed problem — expensive one.

Don't attempt full rewrite running parallel to old system. Old keeps changing, copy never catches up, almost always fails.

Better: progressive strangling. Put router in front, move one piece at time, keep old authoritative until new piece verified.

Weigh cost of microservices honestly — each service wants releases, monitoring, logs, alerts, someone who owns it. Below five devs, operational cost almost always beats benefit.

Consider modular monolith — option nobody brings up. Sharp internal boundaries, one release, still option to extract module day it's genuinely needed.

Real deciding question isn't technical: what must team do in year that it can't do now. Answer that first, architecture follows from it.
