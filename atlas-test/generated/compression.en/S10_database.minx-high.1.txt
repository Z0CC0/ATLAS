Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload demands it and you have numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably but none outstandingly. It has real transactions, which you can't do without on orders. Its built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. Analytics are fine up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL is equivalent on orders but weaker on data types and complex queries.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier with variable schemas. Multi-document transactions arrived late and cost a lot, which makes it a risk for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search, with relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. It isn't an authoritative store: it has to be fed from another database, not replace it. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster. It isn't for orders because point updates are its weak spot.

The usual setup once you grow is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it.

Each system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost isn't the licence but the time of the people keeping those systems running.
