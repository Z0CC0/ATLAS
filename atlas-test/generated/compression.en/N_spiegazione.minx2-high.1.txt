Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree right away, but converge within finite time if writes stop.

Why care: it hits whoever reads right after writing. User posts comment, reloads, comment missing.

Trade is against availability: strong consistency must refuse requests when replicas cannot talk.

Typical convergence: under a second within same region, seconds across regions.

Common fix: read from primary replica for session that just wrote, not for everyone.

Not a defect to eliminate but a choice. Most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

Cases where it's unacceptable are few and recognisable: account balances, seat availability, identifier uniqueness.
