Postgres, for you.

Table already handle up to ~1000 jobs/minute with SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED. Small project not hit that ceiling.

Job live in same transaction as your data write. Data roll back, job never fire — no separate system to keep in sync, no dual-write bug.

Persistence free. Process die, jobs still there, no config needed.

Redis faster (sub-millisecond) and handle way more volume, but: one more service to run/monitor/upgrade, and persistence not automatic — without AOF enabled, restart wipe queued jobs.

For small project, cost of Redis (extra ops burden, extra failure mode) buy nothing you need yet. Move to Redis when volume actually demand it, not before.
