Start from what the team must be able to do a year from now that it can't do today. That question isn't technical, and it decides the rest.

Then find which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Next, where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Avoid a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old one keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Use progressive strangling instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it.

Below five developers, that operational cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It has sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
