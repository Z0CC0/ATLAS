Not worth it. On three distinct values the planner treats it as low cardinality and skips the B-tree — a sequential scan wins whenever the filter matches more than roughly 5-10% of rows, which with only three values it almost always does. Meanwhile the index still costs you on every write and takes disk space, for no read benefit.

The one case where it pays off: a partial index on just the rare value, if one of your three values is genuinely uncommon. That the planner will actually use.
