Start with what your team must be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today. The question that settles this isn't technical, and the rest follows from the answer.

Next, find which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split, so leave them alone.

Then map where transactions start and end. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem, so draw service lines where transactions already stop.

Don't do a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old one keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle the old system progressively instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. Keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Count the operational cost before choosing microservices. Every service wants releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider **modular monolith**, the option nobody mentions. It gives you sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can still extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
