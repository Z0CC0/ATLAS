2008: paper appears, signed Satoshi Nakamoto, pseudonym never identified. Title: *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System*. Problem tackled: double spending without central body keeping ledger.

Solution: chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Proof of work makes rewriting past expensive — redoing it means redoing every later block too.

First block generated 3 January 2009, contains headline of Times article on bank bailout — works as timestamp and statement of intent both.

Block reward starts at 50 bitcoin, halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly four years each. Total issued tends toward 21 million, no further.

First documented commercial transaction: 2010, two pizzas paid with 10,000 bitcoin.

Satoshi stops participating publicly in 2011; those bitcoin never moved since.

Project grows out of cypherpunk scene, after earlier attempts — b-money, bit gold — that didn't solve ordering.
