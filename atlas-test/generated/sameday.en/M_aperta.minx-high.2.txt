Start with a non-technical question: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Let that answer choose the architecture.

1. Find which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

2. Find where the transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Avoid a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Use progressive strangling instead. Put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. With fewer than five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It has sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
