After a write, replicas disagree for a while, then converge within a finite time once writes stop.

It matters to anyone who reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads the page, and the comment isn't there.

Common fix: read from the primary replica, but only for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

Convergence takes under a second between replicas in the same region, and seconds across regions.

The tradeoff is availability. A system that demands strong consistency has to refuse requests when its replicas can't reach each other.

It's a choice, not a defect to eliminate. Most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

It's unacceptable in only a few recognisable cases: account balances, seat availability, and uniqueness of an identifier.
