Before anything technical: what does the team need to be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? That question decides the architecture.

1. Which parts actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split out.

2. Where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Avoid a full rewrite running parallel to the old system. It almost always fails, because the old one keeps changing and the copy never catches up.

Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front and move one piece at a time. The old system stays authoritative until the new piece is verified.

Microservices cost more to operate. Every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and an owner. Below five developers that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Consider a modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It has sharp internal boundaries and one release. You can still extract a module the day you actually need to.
