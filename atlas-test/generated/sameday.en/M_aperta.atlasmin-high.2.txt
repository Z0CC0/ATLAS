**What must your team be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today?** That question decides the architecture more than any technical one.

Then check these:

Which parts of the monolith actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split out.

Where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem, so draw service lines around those boundaries, not across them.

Team size. Microservices cost more to operate: every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

The modular monolith, the option nobody mentions. It gives you sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can extract a module the day it is genuinely needed.

How you migrate. A full rewrite running in parallel with the old system almost always fails, because the old system keeps changing and the copy never catches up. Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until the new piece is verified.
