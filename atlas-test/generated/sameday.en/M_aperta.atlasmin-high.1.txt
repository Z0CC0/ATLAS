The question that decides it isn't technical: what must your team be able to do a year from now that it can't do today? Answer that first, and the architecture follows from it.

Then map two things in the monolith:

Which parts actually change. Stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction spanning two services becomes a distributed problem.

Don't do a full rewrite running in parallel with the old system. It almost always fails: the old one keeps changing, and the copy never catches up. Strangle it progressively instead. Put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until the new one is verified.

Microservices cost more to run. Every service wants releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

Also consider the **modular monolith**, the option nobody mentions. It has sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.
