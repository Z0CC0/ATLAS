**Recommendation:** start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse later, only when a specific workload needs it and you have numbers to prove it.

**Postgres**
- Handles all four workloads acceptably. None of them outstandingly.
- Orders: real transactions. On orders these are not negotiable, and this decides the base store.
- Catalogue: fine.
- Text search: built in. Good enough below 1 million documents, weak above.
- Analytics: fine up to tens of millions of rows. Past that, scans get slow.

**MySQL**
- Equivalent to Postgres on orders.
- Weaker on data types and complex queries. It gains you nothing over Postgres.

**MongoDB**
- Variable schemas make the catalogue easier.
- Multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly. That makes it a poor base when orders are on the platform.

**Elasticsearch**
- Far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, tolerance of typos.
- It is not an authoritative store. You feed it from another database; it never replaces one.
- Below 1 million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

**ClickHouse**
- Far better at analytics. Columnar scans are orders of magnitude ahead.
- Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

**Usual final setup:** Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Get there step by step, not on day one.

**Cost of each system you add:**
- A synchronisation problem.
- A data alignment problem.
- People's time to keep it running. That is the real cost, not the licence.

**When to add each one:**
- Elasticsearch: the search corpus grows past about 1 million documents and Postgres search gets weak.
- ClickHouse: analytics grows past tens of millions of rows and scans get slow.
- Neither threshold reached: stay on Postgres.
