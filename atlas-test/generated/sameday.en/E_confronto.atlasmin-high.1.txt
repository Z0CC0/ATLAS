**Postgres**, then Redis once volume really needs it.

Postgres with `SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` handles up to roughly a thousand jobs per minute.

The job sits in the same transaction as the data, so it never starts when the matching write rolls back.

Persistence comes free: the queue survives if the process dies.

Redis handles far more volume, with latency under a millisecond.

But it's one more service to run, monitor and upgrade. Persistence needs configuring too: without AOF enabled, a restart loses queued jobs.
