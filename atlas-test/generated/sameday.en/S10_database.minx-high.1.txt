Start with **Postgres alone**. Add another system only when one workload needs it and you have the numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably but none of them outstandingly. It has real transactions, which orders can't do without. Its built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, and past that the scans get slow.

MySQL is equivalent on orders but weaker on data types and complex queries.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier because it allows variable schemas. Multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly, which counts against it for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and tolerance of typos. It is not an authoritative store, so it has to be fed from another database rather than replace one. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, because its columnar scans are orders of magnitude faster. It is not for orders, since point updates are its weak spot.

If you outgrow Postgres alone, the usual setup keeps Postgres as the authoritative store and feeds Elasticsearch and ClickHouse from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. The real cost is not the licence but **the time of the people keeping it running**.
