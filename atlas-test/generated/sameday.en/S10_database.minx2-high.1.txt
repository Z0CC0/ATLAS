**Start with Postgres alone.** Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload demands it, and you have numbers to show it.

Postgres handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. It has real transactions, which orders can't do without. Built-in text search is good enough below a million documents and weak above that. Analytics hold up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL is equivalent on orders but weaker on data types and complex queries.

MongoDB makes the catalogue easier with variable schemas. But multi-document transactions arrived late and are costly, which is a problem for orders.

Elasticsearch is far better at text search: relevance, synonyms and typo tolerance. It isn't an authoritative store, so it has to be fed from another database, not replace it. Adding it below a million documents is almost always premature.

ClickHouse is far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude faster. It isn't for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

The usual setup when you grow is Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Every system you add brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **The real cost is not the licence but your team's time keeping each system running.**
