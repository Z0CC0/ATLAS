Bitcoin grew out of the cypherpunk scene. Earlier attempts, b-money and bit gold, did not solve ordering.

2008: paper "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" appears, signed Satoshi Nakamoto, a pseudonym never identified. Problem it tackles: double spending with no central body keeping the ledger. Solution: chain of blocks ordered by proof of work. Rewriting the past is expensive, since the work must be redone for every later block.

3 January 2009: first block generated, carrying headline of Times article on bank bailout. Works as both timestamp and statement of intent.

Block reward starts at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, roughly four years. Total issued tends to 21 million, no further.

2010: first documented commercial transaction, two pizzas paid with 10,000 bitcoin.

2011: Satoshi stops taking part publicly. Satoshi's bitcoin have never been moved.
