Start with Postgres alone. Add Elasticsearch or ClickHouse only when a specific workload proves it needs one, with numbers in hand.

**Postgres**: handles all four workloads acceptably, none outstandingly. Real transactions, which orders can't do without. Built-in text search good enough below a million documents, weak above. Analytics fine up to tens of millions of rows, then scans get slow.

MySQL: equivalent on orders, weaker on data types and complex queries.

MongoDB: variable schemas make the catalogue easier, but multi-document transactions arrived late and cost.

Elasticsearch: far better at text search: relevance, synonyms, typo tolerance. Not an authoritative store. It has to be fed from another database, not replace it. Below a million documents, adding it is almost always premature.

ClickHouse: far better at analytics, with columnar scans orders of magnitude ahead. Not for orders, because point updates are its weak spot.

Usual end state: Postgres as the authoritative store, with Elasticsearch and ClickHouse fed from it. Each added system brings a synchronisation problem and a data alignment problem. **Real cost of specialised systems**: not the licence, but the time of the people keeping them running.
