Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas don't agree right away, but they converge within a finite time once writes stop.

It matters to anyone who reads right after writing. A user posts a comment, reloads and doesn't see it.

Common fix: read from the primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

Typical convergence: under a second across replicas in one region, seconds across regions.

The trade is against availability. A system demanding strong consistency must refuse requests when replicas can't talk.

It's a choice, not a defect to eliminate. Most systems that scale accept it deliberately.

Unacceptable only in a few recognisable cases: account balances, seat availability, uniqueness of an identifier.
