**The deciding question isn't technical.** Ask what your team must be able to do a year from now that it can't do today, and choose the architecture that makes that possible.

Before choosing, check these:

**Which parts of the monolith actually change.** The stable parts gain nothing from being split.

Where the transaction boundaries sit. A transaction that spans two services becomes a distributed problem.

How big your team is. Microservices cost more to run: every service needs releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. Below five developers, that cost almost always exceeds the benefit.

The modular monolith, which nobody mentions. It has sharp internal boundaries and one release, and you can extract a module the day it's genuinely needed.

How you'll migrate. A full rewrite running in parallel with the old system almost always fails, because the old one keeps changing and the copy never catches up. Strangle it progressively instead: put a router in front, move one piece at a time, and keep the old system authoritative until the new one is verified.
