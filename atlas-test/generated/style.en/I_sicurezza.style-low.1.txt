No. Anything that reaches the browser is readable, and obfuscation falls apart the moment someone opens the developer tools or looks at the network requests.

The key belongs on the server. The frontend calls an endpoint on your own backend, and that endpoint calls the service with the key. Put a rate limit on that endpoint, or it becomes a free way for anyone to use your key.

If the key has already shipped in the frontend, treat it as compromised and rotate it at once. Removing it from the code is not enough: it stays in the repository history, and anyone with a clone already has it.
