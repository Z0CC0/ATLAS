No. Anything that reaches the browser is readable. Obfuscation falls apart the moment someone opens the developer tools or looks at the network requests.

The key belongs on the server. Your frontend calls an endpoint on your own backend, and that backend calls the service with the key. That endpoint needs a rate limit, or it becomes a free way for anyone to use your key.

If the key has already been published, treat it as compromised and rotate it at once, not merely remove it from the code. Taking it out of a commit is not enough: it stays in the repository history, and anyone with a clone already has it.
