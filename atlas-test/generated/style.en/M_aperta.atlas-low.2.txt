Start from what actually changes. Stable parts of monolith gain nothing from being split, so map which code moves often and which has not been touched in years.

Then find where transaction boundaries sit. A transaction that today spans two tables inside one PHP process becomes a distributed problem the moment those tables live behind two services.

Do not plan a full rewrite running in parallel with old system. It almost always fails: old one keeps changing, copy never catches up. Alternative is progressive strangling: router in front, move one piece at a time, old system stays authoritative until new piece is verified.

Count your team before choosing microservices. Every service wants releases, monitoring, logs, alerts and a person who looks after it. Below five developers that operational cost almost always exceeds benefit.

Option nobody mentions: modular monolith. Sharp internal boundaries, one release, and ability to extract a module the day it is genuinely needed, not before.

Deciding question not technical: what must team be able to do a year from now that it cannot do today. Answer that first, architecture follows from it.
