Eventual consistency: after a write, replicas do not agree immediately, but converge within finite time if writes stop.

Trade against availability. System demanding strong consistency has to refuse requests when replicas cannot talk. Most systems that scale accept eventual consistency deliberately: not a defect to eliminate but a choice.

Why you should care: it bites whoever reads right after writing. User posts a comment, reloads, does not see it. Typical convergence across replicas in same region under a second, across regions rises to seconds. Common fix: read from primary replica for the session that just wrote, not for everyone.

Cases where it is unacceptable are few and recognisable: account balances, seat availability, uniqueness of an identifier.
