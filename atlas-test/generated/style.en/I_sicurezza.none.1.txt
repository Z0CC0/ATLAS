No. Anything that reaches the browser is readable. Obfuscation falls apart the moment someone opens the developer tools or looks at the network requests, because the key has to be sent in the clear for the call to work.

The key belongs on the server. Your frontend should call an endpoint on your own backend, and that endpoint calls the service with the key. Put a rate limit on that endpoint, or it becomes a free way for anyone to use your key.

If the key has already been published, even in an obfuscated form, treat it as compromised and rotate it at once. Removing it from the code is not enough. Taking it out of a commit still leaves it in the repository history, and anyone with a clone already has it.
