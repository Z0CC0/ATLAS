For a small project, use Postgres. You already run it, and a queue built on SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED carries roughly a thousand jobs a minute, which is more than a small project needs.

Two things make it the better fit at this size:

- **The job lives in the same transaction as your data.** If the write that enqueued it rolls back, the job never starts. With a separate queue you have to handle that case yourself.
- **Persistence comes free.** If the process dies, the queue is still in the table.

Redis wins on raw performance. It handles far more volume and answers in under a millisecond. But it costs you one more service to run, monitor and upgrade, and persistence is not on by default. Without AOF enabled, a restart loses every queued job.

Start on Postgres and move to Redis only when your volume genuinely demands it.
