No.

Anything that reaches the browser is readable. Obfuscation falls apart the moment someone opens the developer tools or looks at the network requests.

Keep the key on the server. The frontend calls an endpoint on your own backend, and that endpoint calls the service with the key.

Put a rate limit on that endpoint, or it becomes a free way for anyone to use your key.

If the key has already been published, treat it as compromised and rotate it at once. Removing it from the code is not enough. Taking it out of a commit does not help either: it stays in the repository history, and anyone with a clone already has it.
